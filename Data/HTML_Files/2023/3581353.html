Inflatable systems have been attracting attention in the field of interaction design. Conventional tabletop-sized pneumatic systems tend to be complex because they require bulky and noisy equipment. Therefore, several liquid-to-gas phase change actuators that use vaporization have been proposed. But these actuators have problems with controllability, reusability, and reconfigurability. In this study, we propose InflatableMod, novel inflatable modules based on the efficient control of liquid-to-gas phase change actuators. These are designed with a compact circuit that has a liquid transfer function to feed the required amount of low-boiling-point liquid into the pouch and a heating function to inflate the pouch by the volume change. This approach allows for a compact, silent, and untethered inflatable system. It is also possible to create an untethered and reconfigurable multi-inflatable system because each module is synchronized. In this paper, we propose the design of the modules, evaluate their performance, and present application scenarios.

1 INTRODUCTION

Shape-changing interfaces have long attracted attention because they can dynamically change the shape of substantial objects. Various studies on shape-changing interfaces have their roots in tangible [ 42 ] or organic user interfaces [ 21 ]. They draw on expertise from various disciplines. Recent developments on shape-changing interfaces are not only in the field of HCI but also in engineering, architecture, behavioral science, design, and many other fields, including comprehensive studies of grand challenges [ 4 ] or affordances [ 34 ].

Particularly, inflatable shape-changing interfaces have the advantage of being freely changeable in volume. Various inflatables have been proposed, ranging from general-purpose interfaces [ 28 , 32 , 40 , 53 ] with dynamic shape changes to specific applications [ 1 , 19 , 22 , 30 , 41 ] such as haptic sensation, portable mobility, or soft robotics. In addition, shape-changing interfaces [ 10 , 14 , 16 , 45 , 46 , 47 , 48 , 54 ] that construct structures using multiple inflatables have been proposed as high-potential applications because they enable more complex shape changes by increasing the number of inflatable bladders.

However, there are still many challenges for inflatables in practical applications. Conventional pneumatic systems usually use air compressors for large-sized systems and commercial pneumatic pumps for tabletop-sized systems as the drive source. Both require external equipment as a drive source to feed air through tubing and are not self-contained systems. Most of these drive sources are noisy. In addition, most previous studies use active valves for pneumatic control, which further increases the drive noise on top of the drive source. Furthermore, the conventional multi-inflatable system requires separate tubing and active valves for each bladder to allow air to flow in and out of each inflatable. These extra setups make the entire system bulky and complex, limiting the layout of the placement.

Several liquid-to-gas phase change actuators [ 3 , 17 , 18 , 27 , 29 , 50 ], which utilize the volume change that occurs during the vaporization process, have been proposed in recent years. These actuators are not bulky owing to the absence of tubing, and can be driven silently and flexibly. However, most existing liquid-to-gas phase change actuators are made from small flat pouches, and three-dimensional pouches such as cubes are rarely made due to heating efficiency challenges. Furthermore, it is difficult to control heating by phase change. It is limited to discrete on/off states, either fully operational or fully stationary, making it difficult to inflate programmatically to the middle states. Additionally, the module can be used only a limited number of times. Reconfigurability also needs to be improved because the design cannot be changed once the liquid is filled in advance. Thus, there are still many challenges in order to utilize liquid-to-gas phase change actuators in practical inflatable applications.

In this paper, we propose InflatableMod, novel inflatable modules based on the efficient control of liquid-to-gas phase change actuators. These are designed with a compact circuit that has a liquid transfer function and a heating function. InflatableMod consists of Phase-change Control Module and Pouch Module. The Phase-change Control Module generates liquid flow and feeds a low-boiling-point liquid into the Pouch Module. The temperature of the liquid is controlled by a Peltier device, which causes the Pouch Module to inflate by the volume change of the phase change. We also utilize electrohydrodynamic (EHD) pumps [ 5 , 6 , 7 , 9 , 15 , 24 , 33 ], based on the EHD phenomenon [ 35 , 36 , 38 ], for the pump function to generate liquid flow because of their ability to drive quite silently. In addition, it has a high degree of freedom of placement and can be embedded into objects in different shapes. The EHD phenomenon generates liquid flow when a high voltage is applied to a dielectric liquid. Using an EHD pump, the liquid flow is generated simply by placing an electrode in the liquid and applying a voltage, eliminating the need for mechanical moving parts. Furthermore, by feeding only the required amount of low-boiling-point liquid into the Pouch Module and then efficiently controlling the heating, the previously-mentioned drawbacks of existing liquid-to-gas phase change actuators can be mitigated and resolved. This feature enables the creation of inflatable systems of various shapes and sizes by liquid-to-gas phase change, as well as control in the middle range. In addition, as each module is wirelessly synchronized, it is possible to create a multi-inflatable system in which each inflatable structure is untethered and can be reconfigured for placement. This allows inflatables to be placed anywhere without having to consider the location of tubing and valves, eliminating the limitation of the module layout and system complexity that have been the challenges for multi-inflatables.

In this study, we present the module design, evaluate the performance of InflatableMod as an inflatable system, and show the possible application scenarios. The core novelty of this paper lies in the first exploration of the novel inflatable approach in tabletop-sized inflatables free from extra device requirements, tubing constraints, and noises. In summary, we contributed:

  1. An efficient liquid-to-gas phase change control that silently inflates three-dimensional pouches for tabletop-sized inflatables.
  2. A liquid-to-gas phase change control that changes an inflatable's volume continuously by feeding the pouch only with the required amount of liquid.
  3. Design and fabrication pipeline of InflatableMod that allow for easy replication.
  4. A technical evaluation of performance and quietness as an inflatable system.
  5. Novel application scenarios enabled by silent, untethered, and reconfigurable inflatables.

2 RELATED WORK

2.1 Pneumatic Shape-Changing Interfaces

Pneumatic shape-changing interfaces have been actively studied in various fields, mainly in object deformation and tactile feedback, owing to their advantage of freely changing volume.

Various interfaces with dynamic shape changes have been proposed, ranging from versatile to application-specific. Various general-purpose methods are available. PneUI [ 53 ] has several pneumatic prototypes that render curvature, volume, and texture. AeroMorph [ 32 ] proposed a textile-based design space that could be folded into various shapes using multiple heat-sealing methods. Printflatables [ 40 ] is a design and fabrication system for human-scale, functional and dynamic inflatable objects. MorphIO [ 28 ] combines inflatable modules with built-in deformation sensing to provide soft actuators with interactions. Various studies have been conducted on specific applications. For use in mobility, poimo [ 30 , 41 ], a portable and inflatable mobility device with a mechanism made of a mass-producible material called drop-stitch fabric, has been proposed. Artificial muscles using air pressure have also been proposed, woven into fabric [ 19 ] and applied to muscles with various characteristics and shapes [ 1 ]. OmniFiber [ 22 ] is proposed as a soft-wire-based material system for designing motion-based interactions for on-body and on-object applications. Several toolkits have also been released in recent years to lower the barrier of entry for handling gases. Some of the best-known ones are The Soft Robotics Toolkit [ 20 ], Pneuduino [ 31 , 37 ], Programmable Air [ 2 ], MorpheesPlug [ 23 ], and FlowIO [ 44 ], which have been a great help in pneumatic prototyping.

As mentioned above, various pneumatic interfaces have been proposed. As the drive source, air compressors are used for pneumatic modules in larger-sized pneumatics systems [ 8 , 10 , 32 , 40 , 45 , 46 , 48 , 53 ], while commercial pneumatic pumps such as diaphragm pumps are often used for tabletop-sized ones [ 14 , 22 , 28 , 47 , 54 ]. The toolkits [ 2 , 20 , 23 , 31 , 37 , 44 ] described above also use commercially available pneumatic pumps. Air compressors are large and loud. Commercial pumps usually use solenoid valves and active valves, making the pump noisy as well. In terms of inflatable interfaces, noises tend to be distracting and irritating to users and affect user experience. To address these challenges of tabletop-sized inflatables, we present a novel approach to inflatables that does not require external equipment and is driven with no noise.

2.2 Multi-Inflatable Shape-Changing Interfaces

There are not only single-inflatable shape-changing interfaces but also multi-inflatable shape-changing interfaces that use multiple inflatable structures. Multi-inflatable shape-changing interfaces allow for more complicated shape change by increasing the number of inflatable bladders; therefore, a study with high potential for application has been proposed.

TilePop [ 48 ], which consists of cubic airbags arranged in a 2D array with a folding structure, is an interface for whole-body interactions in virtual reality. Similarly, LiftTiles [ 45 , 46 ] are building blocks for prototyping room-scale shape-changing interfaces utilizing an array of inflatable actuators. The Force Jacket [ 10 ] has 26 airbags mounted on the jacket, presenting complex tactile feedback throughout the upper body. COLORISE [ 14 ] is a display that can deform and change the color of objects arranged on pixels by stretching an elastomer membrane under air pressure. PuPoP [ 47 ] is a lightweight pneumatic shape-proxy interface worn on the palm and uses one or more inflatables to represent handheld objects in a VR environment. Bubble [ 54 ] is a wearable device that allows one to grasp objects by attaching multiple inflatable units to the fingers. Layer by Layer [ 16 ] introduces a layer-driven design for soft surfaces that enables the production of dynamic, discretely actuated surfaces at multiple scales.

Thus, various multi-inflatable shape-changing interfaces have been proposed for room-scale applications, virtual reality, and human interaction. However, tubing and active valves are required for each inflatable structure to move air in and out of each inflatable separately when driving multi-inflatables. The need for considering the location of tubes and valves limits the freedom in inflatables’ location allocation and system's layout design. Although many challenges remain for multi-inflatable systems, few previous studies have addressed them. PneuSeries [ 8 ] alleviates layout constraints in a multi-inflatable structure with bidirectional check valves that passively regulate airflow into and out of adjacent inflatables. Some toolkits [ 2 , 20 , 23 , 31 , 37 , 44 ] mentioned in Section 2.1 expand the system and use the pneumatic drive source as a multi-channel. However, the former [ 8 ] has limitations on the sequential inflation order of each inflatable and does not allow synchronized control of all inflatables. Those toolkits that use the pneumatic drive source as a multi-channel system require tubes and valves for each inflatable structure. Hence, it remains a complicated and limited multi-inflatable system.

To address these challenges of tabletop-sized multi-inflatables, we introduce a novel approach to multi-inflatables that is untethered by tubing and allows simultaneous control and free placement of each inflatable structure.

2.3 Liquid-to-gas Phase Change Actuators

Liquid-to-gas phase change actuators utilize the volume change of the vaporization. Various types of liquid-to-gas phase change actuators have been proposed, ranging from engineering research to applied use.

Some engineering researches have been conducted. A flexible thin actuator, which is an encircling actuator consisting of a plastic sheet, a low boiling point liquid, and a flexible heater, is proposed [ 3 ]. Flexibility is achieved using a flexible heater composed of a functional liquid with carbon nanotubes [ 50 ]. Moreover, there is an electric phase-change actuator [ 27 ], which is a printable fluid actuator controlled by an inkjet-printed electric heater. Liquid Pouch Motors [ 29 ] is a family of printable soft actuators that consist of one or more gas-tight bladders filled with a low-boiling-point liquid. Laser Pouch Motors [ 18 ] has been proposed as a method to evaporate small amounts of low-boiling-point liquid using heating by laser projection. Micro Elastic Pouch Motors [ 17 ] is elastically deformable and miniaturized soft actuators that use liquid-to-gas phase change, in which a low-boiling-point liquid is enclosed in an elastic rubber pouch. Several applied uses have also been proposed. milliMorph [ 25 ] proposes a design space and manufacturing system for creating millimeter-scale fluid chambers and channels. Smart Thermally Actuating Textiles [ 39 ] are smart textiles that can shrink or expand in response to temperature. Hapouch [ 51 , 52 ] is a wearable tactile display device that uses a liquid-to-gas phase change actuator and a Peltier device.

Although various liquid-to-gas phase change actuators have been proposed, there are still practical challenges. Most existing liquid-to-gas phase change actuators are made in small flat pouches, and few three-dimensional pouches such as cubes are made. Precise inflation of 3D pouches is a challenge because the speed of vaporization and efficient heating methods must be considered. Furthermore, heating control to vaporize is difficult to achieve the desired volume, even if a closed-loop feedback is applied to achieve the targeted temperature. Therefore, vaporazation control is limited to only discrete on/off states, and it is challenging to control at intermediate states. Because of the difficulty of volume control, previous works have been conducted using only one liquid-to-gas phase change actuator, and simultaneous control over multiple actuators has rarely been possible. In liquid-to-gas phase change actuators, a low-boiling-point liquid called Novec 7000[ 11 ] is commonly used. However, Novec usually contains dissolved air that gradually evaporates into the pouch over time. Since the air cannot escape from the pouch, the Pouch Module inflates over time after repeated use, raising the issue of reusability. There is also an issue of reconfigurability. Conventional liquid-to-gas phase change actuators require the pouch to be filled with liquid in advance and inflates only corresponding to the fixed amount of liquid contained in the pouch. In the sight of fabrication, once the liquid is encapsulated and sealed in a pouch, it is difficult to adjust the inflatable's design afterward.

Hence, we propose efficient phase change control methods to realize continuous control of liquid-to-gas phase change actuators in three-dimensional pouches. Furthermore, the InflatableMod solves the aforementioned issues of reusability and reconfigurability, since the pouches can be freely attached or detached.

3 INFLATABLEMOD

In this section, we describe the system overview of InflatableMod, the efficient phase-change control method, and the deflation method.

3.1 System Overview

InflatableMod consists of inflatable modules for tabletop-sized pneumatic physical interfaces that can be silently driven and freely placed. InflatableMod was designed based on the following requirements for use as versatile pneumatic physical interfaces:

  • It can be driven as a compact, untethered, self-contained module in a simple system configuration that operates without needing bulky, heavy, and noisy equipment.
  • It can be designed to be reconfigurable with inflatables of various shapes and sizes.
  • It can be used repeatedly and can control inflatables in intermediate states.
  • Multiple modules can be controlled wirelessly at the same time.

As shown in Fig. 2 , InflatableMod consists of Phase-change Control Module and Pouch Module. The Phase-change Control Module has a liquid transfer function to generate liquid flow and feed low-boiling-point liquid into the Pouch Module and a liquid heating function to drive the liquid-to-gas phase change efficiently. The Pouch Module is a bladder that can be fabricated and inflated into various shapes and sizes depending on the application.

The operation of the inflatable system with the InflatableMod is conducted in six steps, as shown in Fig. 3 . First, (A) a low-boiling-point liquid is injected into a small tank inside the Phase-change Control Module. Thereafter, (B) the Pouch Module and the Phase-change Control Module are attached together. (C) The inflatable system is then operated remotely using simple GUI software on a smartphone. (D) The liquid heating function of the Phase-change Control Module is then driven to heat the reservoir dish of the Pouch Module. Next, (E) the liquid transfer function is driven to feed the low-boiling-point liquid at an efficient timing to the reservoir dish of the Pouch Module that has just been heated. These operational flows allow the Pouch Module to be inflated by volume change on vaporization. InflatableMod uses Novec 7000 [ 11 ] with a boiling point of 34°C as the low-boiling-point liquid.

Thus, the ability to drive inflatables with such a simple process allows them to be used in a highly versatile manner.

3.2 Efficient Phase-change Control Method

We describe an efficient phase-change control method, one of the features of InflatableMod. InflatableMod proposed new approaches by introducing a geometric structure that facilitates heat transfer and heating control that promotes liquid vaporization.

3.2.1 Reservoir dish's geometric structure that facilitates heat transfer. As shown in the left of Fig. 4 , most of the existing liquid-to-gas phase change actuators heat the low-boiling-point liquid contained in a pouch from the pouch surface. Therefore, the heating efficiency of the liquid is poor because some surfaces are not directly heated by the low-boiling-point liquid. Many approaches to heating have been attempted, including flexible heaters, silver nanoinks, and lasers, but few pouch designs have considered the surface of the liquid to be heated. In contrast, we prepared a reservoir dish such that the liquid is placed only at the heated point, as shown in the right of Fig. 4 . This allows efficient heat to be provided to the liquid, which vaporizes faster, as there is no place for the liquid to settle but at the heated point.

Furthermore, the reservoir dish repeatedly vaporizes the liquid for inflation and receives liquefied gas that flows back into the dish along insides of the pouch. Only the reservoir dish and the Peltier device need to face z-dir, but the other parts can be placed in any orientation, including wall-mounted.

3.2.2 Heating control that promotes liquid vaporization. Moreover, we present an approach for heating control that promotes liquid vaporization. InflatableMod does not require a low-boiling-point liquid to be contained in the pouch in advance. It can feed only the necessary amount of low-boiling-point liquid into the pouch, speeding up the vaporization process. As shown in Fig. 5 , a typical method is first to store the liquid in a reservoir dish and then heat it; however, this method takes time for all liquid to be heated since the volume of liquid per heating surface is large. Therefore, as shown in Fig. 6 , by slowly dropping the low-boiling-point liquid onto the heating surface, the volume per heating surface is significantly reduced, and the liquid vaporizes immediately after dropping. In the first cycle, a small amount of the liquid is fed into the reservoir dish before heating to avoid damaging the Peltier device. If the Peltier device is heated with no liquid, the temperature difference between the heating and cooling surfaces will be too large, and the device will break down. Therefore, it is necessary to first feed a small amount of liquid into the reservoir dish as a heat sink for the Peltier device. From the second cycle, there is always liquid on the heated reservoir dish, so there is no risk of failure due to dry heating.

These two approaches described in 3.2.1 and 3.2.2 improve the operation speed by increasing the efficiency of the vaporization process. Furthermore, this allows the liquid-to-gas phase change to be used in three-dimensional pouches, which has been difficult in the past.

3.2.3 Backflow prevention for programmable check valves with liquid transfer function. The major role of liquid transfer control in InflatableMod is also discussed here. The liquid transfer control delivers liquid to the reservoir dish and significantly contributes as a programmable check valve. The InflatableMod delivers the required amount of low-boiling-point liquid to the Pouch Module, which is then heated to cause a liquid-to-gas phase change that inflates the pouch. However, because there is a flow path to feed the liquid into the pouch, simply heating the pouch causes the gas to flow back when it vaporizes. Therefore, to prevent backflow, the pump is not turned off during heating but is driven at a pressure below the threshold for pushing liquid into the reservoir dish, as shown in Fig. 5 and 6 . The output power of the check valve can be changed dynamically according to the pouch size and the amount of liquid to be injected. The EHD pump can prevent backflow without using a mechanical check valve.

3.3 Deflation Method

InflatableMod has two major deflation methods. The first method is deflation which uses a phase change from gas to liquid. This deflation method is shown in Fig. 7 . The inflation flow is briefly described below (a-1~5). First, from the initial state (a-1), the low-boiling-point liquid is poured into Pouch Module (a-2,3). Then, the backflow prevention control described in 3.2.3 is activated. Because the liquid pushed by the EHD Pump continuously stays in the channel connecting the tank and the pouch, the air cannot escape from the Pouch Module. This is considered as a pseudo-closed-valve state (a-4): the liquid in the channel functions as a stopper of the imaginary valve for sealing the gas inside. The low-boiling-point liquid is heated from the surface of the Pouch Module to induce a phase change and inflates the pouch (a-5). After the heating for inflation is stopped, the backflow prevention control is kept active for deflation operation (a-6). The inside of the pouch keeps the pseudo-close-valve state, and deflation occurs due to the volume change caused by the gas-to-liquid phase change.

The second method is deflation which uses self-weight and negative pressure. Fig. 8 shows the second deflation method. The inflation flow is described (b-1~5). This is the same as the first method (a-1~5). The first step is to attach a self-weight, such as butterfly wings shown in application 6.4 , to the Pouch Module. Then, the liquid is returned to the Pump Unit with the backflow prevention turned off (b-6). Since the liquid no longer blocks the path that the air in the Pouch Module escapes, the inside of the pouch becomes a pseudo-open-valve state. The Pouch Module is first subjected to negative pressure and then to a release state in which no pressure is applied. Therefore, the self-weight causes the pouch to deflate in a short time. At this time, the gas inside the pouch escapes through the ventholes in the Phase-change Control Module's Pump Unit. This method deflates quickly and has a high affinity for representing kinetic motion. The actual deflation time is determined by the weight of the self-weight and the size of the Pouch Module. The heavier the self-weight or smaller the Pouch Module, the shorter the deflation time. In the biomimetic actuator shown in application 6.4 , the self-weight of the butterfly wings is 8 g and the volume of the Pouch Module is approximate 25 mm x 40 mm x 10 mm. The deflation time in this case is approximately 10 seconds. Even if the pouch runs low on liquid after repeated deflation operations, it can be used repeatedly by simply filling it again from the tank in the Pump Unit of the Phase-change Control Module (b-2,3).

4 DESIGN AND FABRICATION PROCESS

In this section, we summarize the design and fabrication process of each module. Subsequently, we describe the untethered and reconfigurable multi-inflatable design and user interface.

4.1 Phase-change Control Module

This subsection first describes the principle of the quiet pump function and then introduces the system design of the Phase-change Control Module.

4.1.1 Principle of Silent Pump Function Utilizing EHD Pump. InflatableMod utilizes EHD pumps, which are characterized by their lightweight and quietness. Among the types of EHD pumps, we adopt EHD pumps with comb-shaped electrodes that produce stable pressure and flow rate. The mechanism diagram in Fig. 9 explains the operating principle of the EHD pump. Comb-shaped electrodes are placed in a dielectric liquid so that the two electrodes, positive and negative, face each other. Then, by applying a high voltage to these electrodes, an electric field is generated between them. As a result of this generated electric field, the dielectric liquid is ionized and moved to the electrode side of the opposite charge, resulting in a swimming phenomenon. This generated electric field ionizes the dielectric liquid. As ions move from cathode to anode of the electrodes, a liquid flow is generated. With this technology, liquid flow can be generated without mechanical parts and is used in this system as a lightweight and quiet pump.

4.1.2 System Design of Phase-change Control Module. Fig. 10 shows the system design of the Phase-change Control Module. The Phase-change Control Module consists of two units: the Pump Unit, which contains the EHD pump, and the Control Unit, which has both a high-voltage control function to drive the Pump Unit and a liquid heating function. During operation, a low-boiling-point liquid is filled in the tank of the Pump Unit, and the Control Unit applies a high voltage of 1 - 5 kV to generate liquid flow with the EHD pump. The Control Unit then controls the liquid heating for the liquid-to-gas phase change. The Control Unit and Pump Unit are electrically bonded by magnets so that the amount of low-boiling-point liquid in the tank can be adjusted based on the application. The design allows for handy replacement.

4.1.3 Electronic Implementation of Control Unit. There is a description of how the Phase-change Control Module is implemented electronically in the Control Unit. The Adafruit QT Py is used as the microcontroller, and the ATSAMD21E18 is used as the main processor, allowing for a compact configuration and the use of the generic Arduino development environment. The high voltage applied to the EHD pump is controlled using an ultra-miniature high voltage DC to DC converter, UMHV0550 (HVM Technology, Inc.). A Peltier device is used to control the liquid-to-gas phase change because of the short rising time in heating. Round-shaped Peltier elements (TEC1-050031026) and square-shaped Peltier elements (TEC1-07103) are mainly used for freedom of placement. The radio function uses the nRF24L01+ (Nordic Semiconductors) 2.4 GHz radio described above. A 2-cell lithium polymer battery is used for the power supply because of its small size and power efficiency. The Control Unit is sufficiently small to fit in the palm of the hand (20 mm × 50 mm × 15 mm).

4.1.4 Fabrication Process of Pump Unit. The Pump Unit is based on the Pump Module of HydroMod [ 26 ], which was presented as constructive modules for hydraulic prototyping. The Pump unit of the proposed InflatableMod is designed to be extended to a built-in small tank. It consists of six layers: a Kapton sheet as the foundation, copper tape for the comb-shaped electrodes that cause the EHD phenomenon, a neodymium magnet as the connection with the Control unit, a VHB sheet as the flow path, an acrylic plate to protect the flow path, and a small tank that contains low-boiling-point liquid. For the connection to the Pouch Module, a silicon tube with high sealing performance and easy handling is used. Therefore, the Pouch Module can be reconfigurable according to the application. The Pump unit is also sufficiently small to fit in the palm (22 mm × 50 mm × 20 mm).

4.2 Pouch Module

In this subsection, we introduce the basic structure and fabrication process of the reusable Pouch Module.

4.2.1 System Design of Pouch Module. Fig. 12 shows the system design of the Pouch Module. The Pouch Module receives a low-boiling-point liquid from the Phase-change Control Module through an acrylic rod. A reservoir dish is connected to the pouch for efficient vaporization. Because the Phase-change Control Module heats directly under the reservoir dish and the liquid is constrained inside the reservoir, highly efficient vaporization is realized.

4.2.2 Fabrication Process of Foldable Pouch. InflatableMod can drive three-dimensional pouches in various sizes and shapes with the same system; therefore, the pouch fabrication process is presented here. Pouches are fabricated from 0.75 mm PE sheet because it is a lightweight and inexpensive material with excellent impact resistance. The PE sheets are processed with a laser cutter(VLS3.60DT) to produce pouches of various shapes based on geometry nets. The processed PE sheet is then folded and sealed using a heat sealer (HS-300). Some examples of the relationship between the pre-assembly geometry net and the post-assembly pouches are shown in Fig. 13 . A hole is drilled in the PE sheet of the same size as the reservoir dish for attachment to the reservoir dish. The regular polyhedron is used for the Pouch Module in InflatableMod as a versatile shape because the shape after assembly will be the same no matter which side the reservoir dish is attached to. Because the reservoir dish is fabricated from acrylic sheets, an off-the-shelf adhesive consisting mainly of silylated urethane resin, which can bond acrylic and polyethylene, is used.

4.3 Untethered and Reconfigurable Multi-inflatable Design

This subsection describes a wireless network that enables untethered and reconfigurable multi-inflatable systems.

4.3.1 Layout configure of multi-inflatable system. As shown in the left of Fig. 14 , most existing multi-inflatable systems require a tube and an active valve for each inflatable structure to inflate separately. This makes the tubes and valves bulky and limits the location of the inflatables in the layout. In addition, because large air compressors or conventional pneumatic pumps are usually used as the driving source, the overall system tends to take up large spaces. To eliminate these challenges, InflatableMod enables an untethered multi-inflatable system, as shown in the right of Fig. 14 .

By embedding a low-power multi-connected wireless design, each inflatable system using InflatableMod can be synchronized. This allows for an untethered multi-inflatable system. Conventional multi-inflatable control was synchronized by attaching tubes and connectors to each pouch, which limited the layout and raised concerns about leakage between the connections of each inflatable. In contrast, InflatableMod can be reconfigured to freely change its placement because it synchronizes individual inflatables wirelessly. This also eliminates the need to consider leakage between each inflatable connection. Furthermore, in a multi-inflatable system, each inflatable is driven independently, so there are no restrictions in controlling any inflatable. In other words, when using multi-inflatable with n inflatables, the number of 2 n states can be created in one step from a state where all pouches are deflated.

4.3.2 System design of wireless communication. Fig. 15 shows the system design of wireless communication. The control signal is sent from user equipment, such as smartphones, to the base station. The data is then aggregated at the base station and sent to each InflatableMod. This mechanism allows each module to be easily controlled from a smartphone. Wi-Fi is used as the communication between the user equipment and the base station. Communication between the base station and each module uses a proprietary radio protocol based on nRF24L01+ for low power consumption, low latency, and multiple connections. The nRF24L01+ can programmatically control output power to suit the application. Even when transmitting at 0 dBm, the highest output state, it consumes only about 12 mA. This is lower power consumption than that of a single LED. It can also generate networks arranged in a tree topology, allowing up to 3125 modules to communicate in both directions, so there is no limit to the number of inflatables in the wireless function.

4.4 User Interface

For the user interface of InflatableMod, we describe the GUI functions below.

The GUI is designed to be intuitive and controlled from a smartphone as user equipment. Assuming no hardware dependency, this GUI software is implemented using an IoT platform called Blynk.

Fig. 16 shows the GUI screen that controls InflatableMod. The GUI consists of three main functions. (a)The first is an individual pump calibration function that sets the upper limit of voltage applicable to the pump in each InflatableMod. (b)The second is an individual pump operation function of each InflatableMod. (c)The third is an individual heating operation function of each InflatableMod. When using it, users can determine the maximum voltage of each pump, and then drive any pump at the desired output as needed. Then, operate the heating function when inflating any module. The UI design allows visual control of pump output and heating output from 0 to 100%. In Fig. 16 , 8 modules can be controlled, but the number of modules can be increased or decreased as desired.

5 EVALUATION

In this section, we describe the evaluation of the basic performance of the inflatable system using InflatableMod, as well as the evaluation of the quietness and continuous operation time, which are the features of InflatableMod.

5.1 Inflating Size

InflatableMod can dynamically vaporize the required amount of low-boiling-point liquid, which allows inflating control in the middle range. The relationship between the volume of the injected low-boiling-point liquid and the volume of gas after the phase change is presented in Fig. 17 . In this experiment, Novec 7000 with a boiling point of 34°C was used as the low-boiling-point liquid. This result indicated that the gas volume increased linearly with the amount of Novec 7000 injected. The pouch did not reach its expansion limit in all the experimented trials. Thus, InflatableMod can create an inflating size in the intermediate state. Further, even when the pouch is fully inflated, the changes in volume can dynamically control the stiffness of the pouch surface, thereby expanding the possibilities for application.

5.2 Inflating Time

For the relationship between inflating time, we conducted experiments by comparing three different methods. The first method is to heat the low-boiling-point liquid in a pouch from outside the pouch by a Peltier device, similar to existing liquid-to-gas phase change actuators. The second method involves storing a low-boiling-point liquid in a reservoir dish and heating the reservoir dish directly using a Peltier device. This method is proposed method 1. The third method is to add drops of low-boiling-point liquid in addition to the second method. The third method uses a reservoir dish as in the second method, and low-boiling-point liquid is dripped into the dish little by little. This method is proposed method 2. In this experiment, Novec 7000 was used as the low-boiling-point liquid.

A comparison of the inflating time results from these three methods is shown in Fig. 18 . Five measurements were taken for each of the three approaches when the injection of low-boiling-point liquid was 0.6 ml and 1 ml, respectively, and the results are shown as box-and-whisker plots. These results shows that the inflating time can be reduced by arranging the reservoir dish so that the liquid is placed only at the heating point. In addition to this approach, it has also been established that dropping the low-boiling-point liquid slightly reduces the volume of the liquid per heated surface, resulting in faster vaporization. This novel approach of structural geometry and heating control alleviates one of the difficulties of conventional liquid-to-gas phase change actuators, namely, the operating speed.

Moreover, it is also indicated that the inflating time is independent from the number of inflatables because each inflatable is driven separately. The inflating time depends on the capacity of each individual pouch module.

This result indicates that the inflating time does not increase with the number of inflatables when creating multi-inflatables. As the system is highly scalable, it has significant potential for various applications.

5.3 Deflation Time

As described in 3.3 , there are two major deflation methods. The first is deflation using phase change from gas to liquid, and the second is deflation using self-weight and negative pressure. Here, we analyze the deflation time of these two methods. For the first type of deflation using phase change, the time from the inflated state to the deflated state was about 20 minutes. The experiment uses a Pouch Module of approximately 43 mm cubic in size at a room temperature of 20°C. For the second type of deflation using self-weight and negative pressure, as shown in the application of the biomimetic actuator, the self-weight causes the pouch to deflate in a short period of time. The actual deflation time is determined by the weight of the self-weight and the size of the Pouch Module. Based on these results, the proposed InflatableMod can be used in two ways. One is to deflate slowly, and the other is to deflate immediately, depending on the application.

5.4 Silent Performance

We evaluate how quiet the InflatableMod is compared to conventional pneumatic systems that use air compressors or pneumatic pumps. We measure the noise level of InflatableMod during the phase change when the module creates a liquid flow. Each measurement was performed in a room with a noise level of approximately 35 dB.

When the InflatableMod was driven, no change in noise level occurred. Generally, the standard for silent notation is 35 dB; therefore, there is no noise when the InflatableMod is driven. Subsequently, the noise level was measured when the air was released from an air compressor maintained at 0.3 MPa. The noise level was approximately 56 dB when releasing air and about 70 dB when the air compressor stored compressed air in the air tank. This noise level is comparable to the interior of a passenger car or the chirping of cicadas, and it can be considered quite noisy. Furthermore, the driving noise of a tube pump (G328) and a diaphragm pump (R385), which are commonly used as compact and palm-sized pumps for tabletop-sized pneumatic systems, were measured to be approximately 55 dB and 56 dB, respectively. The above results indicate that the existing pneumatic system has a high noise level when driven.

Based on these results, the InflatableMod has a high potential for applications in quiet environments and as biomimetic actuators.

5.5 Continuous Mobile Operation Time

We evaluated how long the InflatableMod could run on battery power. The InflatableMod uses a 2-cell lithium polymer battery. We experimented with the time when the EHD pump and Peltier element were running; therefore, the voltage did not fall below 3.0-3.2 V per cell to avoid over-discharging. The running time with a 2-cell 250mAh lithium polymer battery was approximately 24 min, 34 min with a 2-cell 360 mAh lithium polymer battery, and 1h and 11 min with a 2-cell 600 mAh lithium polymer battery. The actual time required to inflate once using InflatableMod is as shown in the results of Section 5.2 , so it can be inflated many times and is sufficient for mobile use. This result indicates that InflatableMod can be driven standalone for a long time. Therefore, they can be used in a mobile environment and increase application's versatility.

6 APPLICATIONS

There are a number of situations that can only be realized with InflatableMod. In this section, we present four application scenarios using InflatableMod.

6.1 Reconfigurable Inflatable Lamp

Many existing pneumatic systems are not designed to be disassembled frequently; therefore, once the system is assembled, it takes a lot of time to reconfigure it. It is also challenging to rebuild existing liquid-to-gas phase change actuators once the liquid is sealed inside the pouch. InflatableMod can be used to create reconfigurable inflatable objects. InflatableMod is divided into Phase-change Control Module and Pouch Module so that users can freely change the shape of the Pouch Module.

Based on the above features, Fig. 19 shows a reconfigurable inflatable lamp that inflates and lights up and can be freely changed in shape according to user's mood. It is suitable for use in quiet places such as libraries or bedrooms. Furthermore, the inflatable lamp takes advantage of the long-time deflation feature of the phase change from gas to liquid. Application scenarios can be envisioned, such as studying until the inflatable lamp deflates, like a physical timer that adapts to the natural environment. It can be used in quiet work and living situations, such as a light for studying or for a nap.

6.2 Continuous data physicalization on a map

Fig. 20 shows continuous inflatable data physicalization on a map. It is possible for the user to arrange multiple InflatableMod in any two-dimensional array and cover them with a map sheet. This allows the map to be visualized in 2.5 dimensions by inflating multiple InflatableMod to match the altitude of each terrain. It further shows the advantage of InflatableMod, which allows inflation in intermediate states. A pouch can be inflated to the desired volume by feeding low-boiling-point liquid into it. Users can decide where to place the InflatableMod and how much volume to inflate. As a result, it is easy to visualize other maps in 2.5 dimensions. In addition to being able to be used repeatedly, this application could potentially be used as an educational tool in the future.

6.3 Dynamically Arrangeable Inflatable Stand

Fig. 21 shows a dynamic arrangement inflatable stand. This inflatable stand consists of a small modular robot, toio [ 49 ], attached to the underside of the InflatableMod. Therefore, each module can be moved dynamically, and can be used with the advantage of being untethered by tubing and can be placed anywhere. Scattered modules gather together in response to the size of various objects to generate an inflatable stand. Since it is combined with the modular function, it can be dynamically generated as a stand for objects of various sizes by arranging each module. Furthermore, the inflatable structure maintains its softness, so it can be used for many objects, including fragile items such as wine glasses or precision machineries. The internal air pressure of the pouch also depends on the pressure of the EHD pump; the InflatableMod pouch pressure is about 8 kPa maximum per pouch, and the output intensity can be controlled. By combining several numbers of InflatableMod, the load is distributed and can withstand loads of much more than 8 kPa.

6.4 Silent Biomimetic Soft Actuator

Fluid-based actuators are often used as biomimetic actuators owing to their soft motion characteristics. However, those general pneumatic systems are often noisy, which hinders soft motion. Existing liquid-to-gas phase change actuators have problems driving soft actuators because they are limited in the size of inflation and cannot be controlled in continuous states. The proposed biomimetic soft actuator using InflatableMod can operate silently and untethered, enabling the expression of movements unique to living organisms. Therefore, it is very compatible with biomimetic actuators.

Fig. 22 shows a silent biomimetic soft actuator made using InflatableMod. It expresses the soft movement of a butterfly. Because the actuator is driven silently, it does not interfere with the natural movement of the butterfly. When the wings of a butterfly rise, they move slowly in conjunction with inflation, and when the wings of a butterfly fall, they move quickly in conjunction with deflation using their self-weight and negative pressure. Here, the butterfly's wings are the self-weight. This inflection of the movement of the wings creates the reality of the butterfly's wing movement.

7 DISCUSSION

In this section, we first describe the durability of the InflatableMod, then the safety of using high voltage and dielectric liquids in the proposed system, followed by a summary of leakage for liquids and gases. Finally, the limitations and future work of the proposed system are presented.

7.1 How Long Will It Last

InflatableMod consists of a Phase-Change Control Module and Pouch Module. The Pouch Module, which inflates into various shapes, is reconfigurable. Therefore, even if the pouch is damaged, it can be used repeatedly by easily replacing the Pouch Module. For the EHD pump used in the Pump Unit of the Phase-Change Control Module, metallic materials are more suitable than non-metallic electrodes for the electrode material in terms of operational stability and lifetime[ 7 ]. Therefore, we used copper as the electrode material because of its high availability and ease of fabrication. The EHD pump of the proposed system fabricated with copper electrodes can operate continuously for hours. Once the pump is fabricated, it can be used for months. Even if the EHD pump is damaged, it can be replaced since the Control Unit and the Pump Unit are connected by magnets. This implies that the entire system can be used repeatedly without having to be rebuilt.

7.2 Safety of the Proposed System Utilizing High Voltage and Dielectric Liquid

When designing new interfaces, it is essential to consider their effects on the human body. The proposal deals with high voltage and dielectric liquids for driving EHD pumps and liquid-to-gas phase changes. The safety aspects of each component are described here.

First, this proposal uses high voltages of 1-5 kV to drive the EHD pump, but there is little danger to the human body from electric shock. Symptoms of electric shock are not caused by voltage but by the amount of current that flows through the body and the location where it flows. According to Matsusada Precision, a manufacturer of high-voltage equipment, 1-5 mA is the same level of shock as static electricity, and 50 mA or more may cause death if the current is energized. The UMHV0550 Ultra-Miniature High Voltage DC to DC Converter used in this proposal has a maximum output current of 100 μA, which is safe enough. Considering safety not only from the power supply but also from the EHD pump, the current generated by the EHD pump is small in principle because the charge X − injected from the electrode is extremely small. Therefore, touching the applied voltage poses no risk to the human body. Furthermore, the proposed module is designed such that the electrodes are not exposed, providing sufficient protection against electric shocks. This topic is also discussed in HydroMod for hydraulic prototyping with EHD pumps [ 26 ].

Additionally, Novec 7000 was used in the proposed system as the dielectric liquid injected into the EHD pump for the liquid-to-gas phase change. According to 3M, which manufactures and sells Novec, it has no effect on the human body, even if it evaporates and leaks into the air.

7.3 Protection against Liquid and Gas Leakage

Measures against leakage are significant in the production of inflatable structures. If leakage frequently occurs at a certain point, the structure cannot function adequately as an inflatable. This proposal addresses liquid leakage in the Pump Unit and gas leakage in the Pouch Modules. First, VHB was used as the material for the flow paths in the pump, where liquid flows and leakage is likely to occur. VHB is made of acrylic resin with high weather resistance and excellent sealing properties; therefore, even if it is used many times, the adhesive strength will hardly deteriorate, and it is unlikely to suffer from liquid leakage. In addition, the acrylic material was sealed using a solvent-type adhesive for acrylic resin plastics. Next, to prevent air leakage, the Pouch Module folds polyethylene sheets and wraps them with a heat sealer to prevent air bubbles from entering the adhesive surface. Therefore, a pouch can be used without worrying about air leakage.

7.4 Distinction over Tabletop-Sized Inflatable System Using Conventional Pneumatic Micropump

In 2.1 and 2.2 we discussed the positioning and contributions of InflatableMod compared to existing pneumatic systems. InflatableMod solves the challenge of tabletop-sized inflatables in that it requires no external equipment, is untethered by tubing, and is silently driven as a self-contained module. However, pneumatic micropumps can also be considered as a pneumatically silent drive method. Therefore, this section summarizes the distinctions between InflatableMod and micropumps. We recognize that pneumatic micropumps are quiet. We also acknowledge that the phase change technique is less energy-efficient than micropumps because it requires heating. However, micropumps have the challenge of being tethered by tubing, which makes the entire system bulkier. Taking the mp6 series (Bartels Mikrotechnik GmbH) as an example, pneumatic micropumps can generally be driven in only one direction. Therefore, to drive the flow in both directions to inflate and deflate, a flow path design using one pump and four active valves or two pumps and two active valves is required, as shown in Fig. 23 . This increases the number of tubing and connections in the flow path, making the entire system complex and bulky. Furthermore, while the micropump alone would drive silently, using an active valve would generate driving noise. Also, micropumps are generally very expensive: the mp6-gas+ (Bartels Mikrotechnik GmbH) [ 43 ], which is also used by omnifiber [ 22 ], costs as much as approximately € 400 per one pump. If we used micropumps for each unit of the six multi-inflatables, such as the application in 6.3 , it would cost € 2400, which is not easy for anyone to handle. From the above, the InflatableMod has unique advantages over micropumps as tabletop-sized inflatables because it is easy to handle without being limited by tubing.

7.5 Limitation & Future Work

We confirmed that the untethered and reconfigurable inflatable systems using InflatableMod are well suited for various situation, but we also discovered some limitations that we will address in future work.

7.5.1 Deflation time. The proposed system uses a phase change approach. Therefore, the deflation time when using phase change from gas to liquid is longer than the conventional pneumatic system. Though deflation using self-weight and negative pressure improves deflation time, here we discuss a solution for deflation time using phase change. This can be solved by implementing a cooling function in the system. In general, a Peltier device's heating and cooling surfaces are reversed in principle when the polarity of the applied voltage is changed. Therefore, we would like to consider a cooling function that does not increase the size of the entire system by using only the existing Peltier device in an H-bridge circuit with MOSFETs instead of using a supplementary cooling element.

7.5.2 Requirement of an upward position of InflatableMod. As mentioned briefly in 3.2.1 , InflatableMod has a limitation in that it has to face upward. If users want to use InflatableMod in a wearable, it would be limited to something that is basically in an upward position, such as placing it on the shoulder. Therefore, in order to enable wearable use that involves intensive movements, we would like to consider a new reservoir dish design that prevents liquids from spilling even if the reservoir dish is tilted, such as a check valve inside the reservoir dish that allows gases to pass through but not liquids, as future work.

7.5.3 Adjustment of drive temperature. In this proposal, Novec 7000 with a boiling point of 34°C was used; however, depending on the application, it may be necessary to drive at different temperatures. Therefore, using Novec 7100[ 12 ], which has a boiling point of 61°C, or Novec 7300[ 13 ], which has a boiling point of 98°C, in the Novec series, the drive temperature can be changed to the respective temperature. Because the boiling point can be simultaneously adjusted by mixing the two liquids, the drive temperature can be determined according to the application.

7.5.4 Interactivity. Because the main focus of this study was on shape-changing in inflatable structures, little consideration was given to methods of sensing user input. We believe that the use of embedded touch sensors or other devices attached to the inflatable structures will improve the interactivity of the user experience. In addition, we would like to consider the use of HMDs in conjunction with VR spaces. This would provide soft feedback that cannot be experienced with mechanical tactile senses, and enable mobile use.

7.5.5 User study. This paper did not conduct a user study. As a next step, we will plan a workshop that includes researchers, designers, and public users. Additionally, we will explore new ways of using multi-inflatable systems now that they can be placed freely on the layout. Furthermore, we will discover new ways of using the inflatable system, such as using it as a new ideation tool, because it is possible to reconfigure inflatable objects.

8 CONCLUSION

In this paper, we proposed InflatableMod, novel inflatable modules based on the efficient control of liquid-to-gas phase change actuators, designed with a compact circuit that has a liquid transfer function and a heating function. This modular design enables compact, silent, and untethered inflatable systems, solving the problems of existing tabletop-sized pneumatic systems that are bulky, heavy, and noisy. In addition, because the locations of tubing and valves no longer need to be considered, the layout restrictions that were considered difficult with multi-inflatable systems can be significantly alleviated. Furthermore, the drawbacks of conventional liquid-to-gas phase change actuators, such as controllability, operation speed, reusability, and reconfigurability, have been mitigated and resolved. Furthermore, we evaluated the performance of the inflatable system and presented novel application scenarios. InflatableMod could be the next step to breaking some new ground in inflatable technology. In the wake of this paper, we hope that various inflatables will become part of our daily lives.
