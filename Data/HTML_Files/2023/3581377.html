Hand-drawn sketches and sketch colourization are the most laborious but necessary steps for fashion designers to design exquisite clothes, especially when the fashion design requires distinctive and personal characteristics from designer style. This paper presents an artificial intelligent aided fashion design system, namely StyleMe, to support the automatic generation of clothing sketches with designer style. Given the clothing pictures specified by the designer, StyleMe can use deep learning based generative model to generate clothing sketches that are consistent with the designer style. The system also supports intelligent colourization on clothing sketch by style transfer, according to specified styles from the real fashion images. Through a series of performance evaluations and user studies, we found that our system can generate effective clothing sketches as good as fashion designers’ human work, and significantly improve the efficiency of fashion design with its sketch colourization method.

1 INTRODUCTION

Fashion is a form of expression that can highlight each person's personality and style [ 29 ]. Traditional fashion design relies on the designer's personal creative sense, which can lead to uncertainty and subjectivity. With the arrival of the big data era and the development of artificial intelligence, fashion design patterns have been changed [ 10 ], where fashion style can be analyzed through techniques such as deep learning on the clothing images and sketches [ 4 , 26 ]. The style of clothing depends on the sketch lines, colours, patterns and details of clothing drawn by the designer. Normally, hand-drawn sketch and sketch colourization are the most laborious but necessary steps for fashion designers to design exquisite clothes, let alone complete a large number of fashion design works.

Given a design theme, designers usually need to browse and explore a large number of clothing pictures related to specific design themes to find clothing materials that can bring design inspiration [ 38 ]. Then they need to arrange the corresponding design materials (e.g., sketch, colours, patterns and details) according to their own design inspiration [ 33 , 42 ]. Despite that some techniques were proposed to automatically generate sketches from images, they mostly focus on extracting the outline of objects in images [ 18 ]. However, clothing sketches usually contain rich line variation; the thickness of these lines and the virtual and real changes can show the three-dimensional sense and texture of clothing styles [ 23 ]. For example, the outer contour of clothing is represented by thick solid lines in clothing sketches, and the pleat and sew lines inside the clothing are represented by thin solid lines in clothing sketches [ 47 ]. The process of fashion design is beyond drawing sketches. Designers also need to select the proper colour in the context of a given clothing sketch to enrich the pattern and style of the clothing [ 21 , 45 ]. For example, typical considerations from designers when creating a fashion design can be “how to match colours for the slim clothing to make the clothing model look more prominent”, and“how to match colours to make men's suits adapt to business scenes and daily life scenes?” [ 25 , 40 ]

Recent advances in generative adversarial networks (GAN) [ 14 ] and artificial intelligence generated content (AIGC) [ 8 ] enable the potentials of artificial intelligence (AI) to assist designers in generating personalized clothing sketches and style transfer from sketch to image. For example, some deep learning models have been successfully applied to sketch synthesis [ 50 ] and style transfer [ 16 ]. However, to the best of our knowledge, no prior research work have tackled the automatic generation of personalized sketches for clothing images and style transfer from sketch to clothing image meanwhile. In order to complete these two functions, three challenges need to be specifically addressed:

  • How to prepare the design manuscripts of fashion designers and a large number of clothing pictures. The drawing process of a clothing sketch is complex and time-consuming, including browsing a large number of clothing pictures, thinking about design inspiration, and drawing clothing sketches with designer style. It requires a large number of clothing pictures and a certain number of designer's freehand manuscripts.
  • How to generate sketches with designer style in large quantities. When designers are required to complete a large number of fashion design manuscripts in a relatively short time, it is usually time-consuming and inefficient. Given a task of drawing clothing sketches, a designer needs to ensure that the clothing sketches have rich semantic information and clear designer style such as the smoothness of lines, the integrity of clothing contours, and the coarseness of lines. These elements can reflect the material and silhouette of clothing.
  • How to quickly colourize the sketch and make the colour combinations harmonious and diverse. During the colour selection, designers first determine the main colour of clothing according to the texture of the sketch, and then choose other colours in some local details to make the clothing more consistent with the design theme, which meets specific aesthetic principles such as elegant colour matching and pleasant clothing silhouette.

To address these challenges, we introduce StyleMe , an intelligent design system for automatically generating the shape and silhouette of clothing sketches and style transfer from sketch to clothing image. The real-world results of intelligent fashion generation with designer style using our StyleMe are presented in Fig. 1 . To prepare the design manuscripts of clothing designers and a large number of clothing pictures, we crawled a large number of clothing pictures without backgrounds from the clothing website to build a picture library for designers to browse clothing pictures and think about design inspiration. At the same time, we invited two professional fashion designers and draw 100 clothing sketches by hand. In the function of automatically generating clothing sketches, given a clothing image specified by the designer, StyleMe imitates the designer's design style by learning the designer's historical manuscripts, so as to generate clothing sketches with the designer's personal style. To enable the system to generate sketches from specific images, a generative adversarial network with channel attention module (CAM) [ 12 ] is employed to learn the style of sketch and content of image. In the function of style transfer, a generative adversarial network with feature decoupling is employed to transfer the style of designer's sketch and additional styles of reference clothing image (e.g. colour selection) into the generated clothing image. StyleMe also incorporates a web-based user interface, from which a user can easily generate sketch of a specific image and modify it to refine its design. The major contributions and novel aspects of this work include:

  • Algorithm. We propose an intelligent framework that can generate fashion sketches with designer style, and at the same time it can transfer the style of sketches according to the user-specified fashion style, so as to generate a variety of fashion pictures with different styles. We use two different generative adversarial networks to realize the two functions in a framework.
  • System. We introduce an intelligent graphic design system, namely StyleMe, to support the interaction process of clothing sketch generation and sketch style transfer. The system displays the specific clothing image, generated sketch, reference clothing images, and generated clothing image after style transfer. Meanwhile, the system allows users to edit the generated sketch by themselves.
  • Evaluation. We demonstrate results from three forms of user studies: (1) a user study verifying the influence of generated sketches with different qualities on sketch style transfer. (2) an efficiency and effectiveness test evaluating whether the generated sketch has the designer's style, and whether the quality of clothing pictures after style transfer is close to or even better than human designers. (3) a feedback user study testing the functions of StyleMe system. The results show that StyleMe can generate clothing sketches with designer style comparable to human designers and generate high-quality clothing images with style transfer as good as human designers. It also proves that our system can improve designer's work efficiency. We also report feedback from professional designers in an expert interview.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section II summarizes the related works. Section III presents our group interview and system overview. Section IV describes the StyleMe system in details. Section V provides experimental results. Section VI gives further discussion and Section VII concludes our work.

2 RELATED WORK

2.1 Data-Driven Creative Support Tools for Design

With the development of digital information technology and the advent of the era of big data, the analysis and utilization of data play an increasingly important role in a variety of industries [ 41 , 43 , 46 ]. In the creative design field, more and more data-driven design support tools have emerged past few years. Some of the tools have been integrated into professional design software or platform. For Instance, Figma design platform provides built-in data analytics in the design system, where users can understand library usage, make updates that matter, and measure the impact of design [ 2 ]. CLO 3D fashion design software uses CLO-SET to download or manage digital contents, which helps to fashion design [ 1 ]. HumanGPS was proposed to incorporate dense human correspondence and try to put digital clothes on directly to human in photos [ 37 ]. Idea Wall offers a software-as-service for collecting ideas online in a structured way, where users can create and publish web pages (“walls”) with one or more columns under which participants add ideas [ 3 ].

Most of the above tools stay in the stage of analysis and management on data itself. How to deeply exploit the value of data in creative design is another area of focus for researchers. Through several case studies of data work in the design group, Kun et al. proposed the design framework Exploratory Data Inquiry to structure data work methodologically and coherently into design processes [ 24 ]. To support the incorporation of fast-emerging technologies in new product design and development, Han et al. introduced a data-driven computational concept generation and evaluation approach for designers in conceptual design [ 15 ]. Based on massive available data, some AI applications started impacting certain design fields. Shi et al. presented EmoG, an interactive system that learns the strokes from the user, suggests potential designs of an intended character, and generates new sketches of the character with six basic expressions [ 35 ]. Xia et al. proposed DataInk, a system that supports the creation of expressive data visualizations with rigorous direct manipulation via direct pen and touch input [ 44 ]. Inspired by these researches, our work focus on providing intelligent support to professional or semi-pro designers in fashion design, our sketch tool not only generate sketch based on the existing dataset, but also try to extract the style feature from user input, to supply personalized content.

2.2 Personalized Sketch Generation

In creative design, especially in the fashion design area, sketch drawing is the fundamental phase during the design procedure. Digital assistance is regarded as a crucial technique to promote the evolution of traditional hand-drawn sketches [ 6 , 27 ]. Matsui et al. presented DrawFromDrawings, an interactive drawing system that provides users with visual feedback for assistance in 2D drawing using a database of sketch images, which allows the retrieval of reference sketches and their interpolation with an input sketch [ 31 ]. Wang et al. presented a novel sketch-drawing method from the raw point clouds, which combines ridge-valley lines and the contour for better expressing the shape information [ 39 ].

In recent years, benefiting from the capability of feature extraction, machine learning-based approaches including Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN), Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and so on, gradually become the mainstream in digital-assisted sketching [ 9 ]. Pix2Pix presented by Isola et al. is a pioneering work in sketch-to-image synthesis area, which utilized Conditional GANs to learn a conditional generative model for image tasks [ 19 ]. Jo et al. presented SC-FEGAN, a novel image editing system consisting of an end-to-end trainable convolutional network, which can generate images as the user provides free-form masks, sketches and colour as inputs [ 20 ]. Chen et al. presented DeepFaceDrawing, a novel deep learning framework for synthesizing realistic face images from rough and/or incomplete freehand sketches, which implicitly models the shape space of plausible face images and synthesize a face image in this space to approximate an input sketch [ 7 ]. Bhunia et al. designed an RL-based framework, which learns to form an optimal stroke subset by interacting with a pre-trained FG-SBIR model. The proposed selector can augment other sketch applications in a plug-and-play manner [ 5 ]. DALL-E 2 is a new AI system released by OpenAI which is based on a two-stage image synthesis model devised by Ramesh et al. [ 32 ]. It can create realistic images and art from a description in natural language. However, how to keep the designer style, one of the human nature in creation, has never been tackled in existing works; most of which can only obtain the outline of the picture, but cannot generate personalized sketches with clear details and painting styles. Besides, existing methods require a large number of hand-drawn sketches to learn, and it is difficult to obtain a large number of pairs of colour maps along with hand-drawn sketches.

2.3 Neural Style Transfer

Neural Style Transfer (NST) refers to a class of software algorithms that process a digital image or video to adopt the appearance or visual style of another image. NST is regarded as an efficient support technique for artists and designers to produce new artwork based on existing style. Different from traditional style transfer methods based on graphics algorithms, NST, as the name suggests, mainly uses neural networks to learn the style feature from existing datasets. Gatys et al. introduced a neural algorithm of artistic style that can separate and recombine the image content and style of natural images. The algorithm allows us to produce new images of high perceptual quality that combine the content of an arbitrary photograph with the appearance of numerous well-known artworks [ 13 ]. Dumoulin et al. investigate the construction of a single, scalable deep network that can parsimoniously capture the artistic style of a diversity of paintings. This network can generalize across a diversity of artistic styles by reducing a painting to a point in an embedding space [ 11 ]. Liu et al. proposed a novel AdaAttN module for arbitrary style transfer. AdaAttN performs feature statistics transferring via modulation with per-point attention-weighted mean and variance of style feature [ 30 ]. Rombach et al. proposed latent diffusion models (LDMs) which turn diffusion models into powerful and flexible generators for general conditioning inputs, and achieve new state-of-the-art scores for image inpainting and class-conditional image synthesis [ 34 ]. However, most of the existing works including currently popular diffusion models are only applied in style transfer of panoramas, which still cannot meet the needs of fashion design, as an engineering process of artistic product, with multi-modal creation on rational and perceptual perspectives. For clothing with clear outlines, the styles of colour and texture are only required to transfer into the outline, rather than cover the entire image. In addition, for clothing design with specific details, many algorithms can only learn the style feature of the original image, but cannot reasonably transfer the colour with specific designer style from the sketch into the generated clothing image, resulting in effect distortion after the clothing generation.

3 GROUP INTERVIEW AND SYSTEM OVERVIEW

We aim to design StyleMe that can generate a corresponding clothing sketch according to the sketch design style of a human fashion designer, and produce a clothing picture according to the colour specified by the designer. To better understand designers’ workflow and design thinking when creating new clothing, we conducted an in-depth group interview with four experienced fashion designers to investigate the general workflow of designing clothing. Based on the interview feedbacks, we develop a web-based user interface as shown in Fig. 2 driven by pre-trained sketch synthesis model and style transfer model for automatically generating sketch pictures and clothing pictures. In this section, we describe the interview procedure, discuss the user feedbacks, and give an overview of the StyleMe system.

3.1 Group Interview

We conducted a group interview with four fashion designers (all females, aged 21–26) in an online face-to-face way. All designers have more than two years of fashion design experience. We set up 15 questions based on the relevant data collected, and asked each designer to answer these questions according to their own design experience. With a goal of understanding the fashion designers’ design process and habits, there is no specific limitation on what designers can answer for the interview. Moreover, we designed a question outline on design decisions of designers from three topics: the general workflow of fashion design, how they search for and select design elements, and the difficulties they have when designing clothing according to their past design experience. During the group interview, we asked the designers based on the question outline, responded to designers’ queries in an interactive way, and recorded the designers’ core insights. At the end of the group interview, we summarized the core insights based on the designers’ feedbacks of the three topics, and these insights were recognized by them, as follows:

Workflow of drawing the clothing sketch. According to the interviewees, there are three common steps they followed when drawing the sketch. First, determine the inspiration source and theme of the design. Second, find materials related to inspiration and theme as a reference. Third, draw the clothing sketch according to the collected relevant materials, and add details for decoration according to the unique elements. Designers have the same requirements for the drawing of clothing sketches, which not only require clear lines but also reflect the structure and details of clothing.

Collecting and selecting design elements. When discussing how to collect and select design elements, designers mentioned that they search for semantically associated objects with the theme. For example, if the theme is determined to be Gothic, they will pay more attention to the elements of dark style when looking for inspiration materials, focus on the relief effect of appearance in the structure drawing of clothing style sketches, and add rivets and other metal objects to the details as decorations. They select the matching design elements by considering the essential tone determined, the rationality of the design elements, and the relevancy to the theme. From the above feedback, we recognize a requirement of considering element relevancy and semantic consistency when selecting the design elements, which inspire our model design.

Design difficulties. All designers agreed that collecting design elements is the most time-consuming and laborious work. Specifically, one designer replied that even if they had certain ideas of what to look for, “ it is not easy to find design elements of high-quality, harmonious colour and suitable pattern ”. Another designer added that “ It is more difficult to find the harmonious combination of design elements. Sometimes expected effect of our design is difficult to achieve properly when proper design elements cannnot be found. ” The designers also brought up another issue that fashion designs are often required to be designed in different clothing styles for different body types. For example, one designer explained that “ people of the same age and preference have to consider different clothing styles depending on their body shape, which can largely influence the choices of clothing style and arrangements of design elements. It is laborious and time-consuming to adjust the design elements when the styles of clothing change. ”

3.2 System Overview

Based on the feedback gathered from the interview, we have developed an interactive user interface for automatically generating sketches and clothing images with designer-specific clothing style. The StyleMe system contains two major functionalities: automatic sketch generation and designer-specific style transfer from sketch to clothing image. Here we first present the design goals extracted from our discussion with designers. Then, we illustrate how we achieve our design goals by introducing each system function.

Design goals. StyleMe is designed based on the following goals. First, StyleMe is designed to relieve designers from the tedious work, such as finding inspiration materials, brainstorming sketch design space and drawing sketch when creating a sketch design from scratch ( G1 ). Secondly, StyleMe can generate sketches similar to the designer's design style according to the inspiration image specified by the designer, which could boost creative inspiration with these sketches ( G2 ). Besides, our model can control the degree of generating sketch details, which depends on whether the designers want to rely on themselves to create the details of clothing sketch or on the system to complete it automatically ( G3 ). Last, our model can transfer sketches to real clothing images of the corresponding styles in the sketches and the reference clothing images specified by designers, which could create multiple design alternatives and ensure the diversity of fashion design ( G4 ).

Automatic clothing sketch generation. After loading the user interface as shown in Fig. 2 , the user can browse a series of clothing images and select a clothing image that can help inspiring their creation. Then StyleMe generates the sketch of the corresponding clothing image. When designing the clothing sketch, the designer should consider the details of waistline, sleeve shape, collar, decoration and so on. It is user-friendly and intelligent to leave this tedious work to the automatic generation model, which could save the designer's effort and allow them to spend more time on creative work. After clicking the button for generation, the clothing sketch can be generated according to the selected clothing image by a pre-trained deep generative model. Fig. 4 gives an overview of the model architecture, which will be detailed in Section 4.3 . It consists of five modules: an encoder module, an adaptive channel feature normalization module, a generator with channel attention module, a loss function module and a discriminator. The encoder module extracts the features from a sketch and a clothing image. The adaptive channel feature normalization module can control the shape and details of the generated sketch. The generator with channel attention module is trained to produce the clothing sketch. The loss function module controls the generated sketch to be closer to the designer's hand-drawn style by minimizing the loss function of the source domain and target domain images. The discriminator enables the generator to generate a more realistic image by discriminating whether the generated image is true. To inspire designers with various design options, we can control the degree of generating sketch details. If a designer needs to generate coarse-grained sketches, the sketches generated by our generator module only include the basic clothing outline, without the dense texture and decoration details of the clothing sketch. Then the designer can paint local areas of the sketches to improve the texture and decoration details of the clothing. If a designer needs to generate fine-grained sketches, the generator can imitate the designer's design style and generate clothing sketches with personalized design details. The designer also can make modifications to the generated sketch through the drawing board. After the designers complete the modification, they can click the “Download” button to save their design work. We will evaluate automatic clothing sketch generation with a user study in the scenario of drawing clothing sketches in following Section 5.2 , so as to see whether this function can relieve designers from the tedious work and generate sketches similar to the designer's designing style.

Style transfer on sketch. In addition to enabling designers to make refinements on the sketch, StyleMe can also transfer specified style from sketch to real clothing image. After the designer determines the sketch design, how to determine the colour matching to reflect the specific design theme is tedious and time-consuming. This function aims to help designers who have difficulty in designing the colour of clothes, which is inspired by the second design difficulty as mentioned by our group interview in Section 3.1 . With this function, users can select a preferred clothing image and click the “Update” button. Then, the sketch can be transferred to a real clothing image with the same colour style as the selected reference clothing picture. Designers can generate multiple clothing images with different combinations of sketch content and colour style by changing their preferred clothing pictures. Fig. 5 gives an overview of the model architecture, consisting of two encoder modules; one extracts content features of the sketch independently and the other extracts the style features of the reference picture, so as to decouple the content features and style features of the generated image. The advantage by doing so is that the content of generated image cannot be disturbed by the reference picture. We will evaluate style transfer on sketch with a user study in the scenario of designing clothing colours according to specific design themes in following Section 5.4 , so as to observe whether this function can improve designers’ design efficiency.

4 STYLEME SYSTEM DESCRIPTION

In this section, we introduce StyleMe, which consists of a data-driven sketch generation model that learns designer's personalized fashion design style given a specified clothing image and a style transfer model that can transfer specified style from sketch to a real clothing image. Specifically, we describe how each of StyleMe's components addresses the design difficulties presented in Section. 3.1 , along with details on system design and implementation.

4.1 Design Space

According to the suggestions given by the designers we have interviewed, design elements involve sketch, colour, pattern and detail in the creative process of fashion design, as shown in Fig. 3 . Correspondingly, we formalize the fashion design into following four dimensions:

  • Sketch. There are two factors that influence fashion design: the human body posture modelling and clothing style modelling. The key to fashion design is to trade-off the relationship between body posture and clothing style by different line styles. Therefore, the sketch quality is the basis of clothing effects.
  • Colour. Colour matching is an important part of fashion design which may be the first element that comes into our sight when we see the clothing sketch, and reflects the idea and soul of designers. Colour on clothing sketch needs to reflect the chiaroscuro based on the relationship between body posture and clothing style.
  • Pattern. In modern fashion design, designers usually draw inspiration from traditional clothing patterns and combine these patterns with popular colours and fashionable fabrics to make clothing. The application of traditional patterns in modern fashion design can adapt to the aesthetic needs of modern society.
  • Detail. Details are the smallest style elements in fashion design. Detail design explores and combines the local shape of the clothing in depth by adding or reducing elements, which can form the overall clothing, enhance the expression of the theme, and increase the appeal of the clothing. Besides, the detailed design gives aura, creativity and beauty to clothing, which can display the style and personality of the clothing.

As mentioned above, StyleMe has been trained to learn the designers’ style based on a number of design works. Specifically, all design works collected are stored in JPG image format and separated into dimensions covering sketch, colour, pattern and detail. Different clothing images are labelled with a fashion category, such as skirts, pants, and T-shirts, so that clothing images from different fashion categories can be trained respectively.

4.2 User Interface

The user interface of StyleMe is composed of two stages: the sketch generation stage and the style transfer stage, as shown in Fig. 2 . In the sketch generation stage, the designer first browses a number of inspirational pictures, selects a favourite one and then the system will automatically generate a sketch of the selected picture on the drawing board. In order to reflect the designer's participation and originality, we have set up two different levels of sketch generation. If designers want to design original clothing details and embellishments by themselves, they only need the outline of the selected inspiration clothing. In this case, they click the “Coarse line generation” button and our system will generate a sketch with the specified clothing outline, but the details are not clear. If designers feel that the sketch generated by the system completely conforms to their design style, they can click the “Fine line generation” button, and then StyleMe will generate a sketch with personalized design style and clear decorative details. In the style transfer stage, designers browse a large number of reference pictures according to their own design themes. They can choose multiple reference pictures they prefer as the target clothing pictures for style transfer. When designers click the “Update” button, StyleMe will transfer the style of sketch based on the selected target image, and generate multiple real clothing images with different colours and styles. These generated clothing images will be presented in a style transfer list. Then designers can choose any of the generated clothing to display on the exhibition board and save their favourite one through the “Download” button.

4.3 Automatic Clothing Sketch Generation

As shown in Fig. 4 , to produce multiple paired sketches for each RGB image, we design a single-direction GAN model which could perform well enough for personalized sketch generation. The sketch generation model is data-efficient. After training a large number of RGB images and sketches once, only a small amount of sketch data are sufficient to generate sketches of related images. The sketch generation stage consists of five modules: an encoder module, an adaptive channel feature normalization module, a generator with channel attention module, a loss function module and a discriminator with channel attention module. The encoder module extracts the features from a sketch and a clothing image and feeds them into a pre-trained VGG [ 36 ] network to encode their feature in batches. The adaptive channel feature normalization module can flexibly select whether to keep the style information of sketches or the content information of clothing pictures by considering the correlation between feature channels and the global statistical characteristics of feature maps. And the generator with channel attention module(CAM) is trained to produce a higher-quality clothing sketch. The loss function module controls the style of the generated sketch to be closer to the designer's hand-drawn style by minimizing the loss function of the source domain and target domain images as well as the content of generated sketch to be more authentic to the original clothing image. The discriminator enables the generator to generate a more realistic image by discriminating whether the feature statistics are from real sketches or generated sketches. In the encoder module, the RGB image tensors are named as X c , the sketch image tensors are named as X s , the generated sketch image tensors are named as X c2s , the pre-trained VGG network is named as Encoder . We extract the features from those images and feed them into a pre-trained VGG network to encode their feature in batches as shown in Eq. ( 1 ).

\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} \mathbf {f_\textrm {content}} &=Encoder(\mathbf {X_{c}}) \\ \mathbf {f_\textrm {sketch}} &=Encoder(\mathbf {X_{s}}) \\ \mathbf {\overline{f}_\textrm {sketch}} &=Encoder(\mathbf {X_{c2s}}) \end{aligned} \end{equation}
(1)

In the channel attention module (CAM), let x ∈ X c , X s represent a pair of samples from the sketch and image domain. Our encoder module is a pre-trained VGG network. Let $\mathbf {E_{c}^{k}(x)}$ be the k-th activation map of the encoded feature from source image and $\mathbf {E_{c}^{k_{ij}}(x)}$ be the value at (i, j). Inspired by CAM, the auxiliary classifier η c is trained to learn the weight $w_{c}^{k}$ of the k -th feature map for the source image, by using the global average pooling and global max pooling. By exploiting $w_c^{k}$ , we can calculate a set of the domain-specific attention feature map. The calculation formulas are shown as Eq. ( 2 ).

\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} \eta _{c}^{x}&=\theta (\sum _{k}w_{c}^{k}\sum _{ij}\mathbf {E_{c}^{k_{ij}}}(\mathbf {x}))\\ \mathbf {a_{c}(x)} &=w_{c}\times \mathbf {E_{c}(x)} \end{aligned} \end{equation}
(2)

Inspired by recent works that use affine transformation parameters in normalization layers and combine normalization functions [ 48 ], we equipped the VGG networks with Adaptive Layer and Instance Normalization(AdaLIN) [ 22 ] whose parameter, γ and β are dynamically computed by a fully connected layer from the attention map. μ I and θ I are instance-wise mean and standard deviation. μ L and θ L are layer-wise mean and standard deviation respectively. γ and β are the parameters generated by the fully connected layer, τ is the learning rate and Δ ρ indicates the parameter update vector determined by the optimizer. The values of ρ are constrained to the range of [0,1] by imposing bounds at the parameter update step. The generator adjusts the value of ρ to be close to 1 in the task where the instance normalization is important and the value of ρ is close to 0 in the task where the layer normalization is important. Therefore, the normalization method AdaLIN as shown in Eq. ( 3 ) has the advantages of selectively keeping or changing the content feature and style feature of $\overline{f}_{target}$ .

\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} AdaLIN(f,\gamma,\beta) &=\gamma \cdot (\rho \cdot \mathbf {\overline{f}_{I}}+(1-\rho) \cdot \mathbf {\overline{f}_{L})} + \beta \\ \mathbf {\overline{f}_{I}} &=\frac{a-\mu _{I}}{\sqrt {\mathbf {\theta ^{2}_{I}}+\epsilon }} \\ \mathbf {\overline{f}_{L}} &=\frac{a-\mu _{L}}{\sqrt {\mathbf {\theta _{L}^{2}}+\epsilon }} \\ \rho &\Leftarrow clip_{[0,1]}(\rho -\tau \Delta \rho) \end{aligned} \end{equation}
(3)

The objectives of loss function are as follows, where Gram is the gram matrix which computes the spatial-wise covariance for a feature map. Firstly the discriminative loss as shown in Eq. ( 4 ) makes sure that X c 2 s is realistic with sketch styles, and the generative loss as shown in Eq. ( 5 ) enables G sketch to generalize well on all kinds of clothing images. Then the mean-square loss ensures the content consistency of X c 2 s to X c . The channel attention module in generative loss as shown in Eq. ( 6 ) updates the weight of different channel features according to the quality of the generated image. The channel attention module in discriminative loss as shown in Eq. ( 7 ) updates the feature attention weight in the discriminator according to the discrimination result of the generated pictures.

4.4 Style Transfer on Clothing Sketch

In the style transfer stage of clothing sketches, as shown in Fig. 5 , the content features of reference image and sketch have different semantic information, but we need to learn style features of the reference image, so we need to decouple style feature and content feature to generate high-quality image. The encoder module can synthesize rich content from a sparse line-sketch by learning the style feature of reference pictures. Our encoder module includes two encoders and a decoder. A style encoder $E_\textrm {style}$ takes in an RGB image to generate a style vector $f_\textrm {style}$ , a content encoder $E_\textrm {content}$ takes in a sketch and extracts a content vector $f_\textrm {content}$ . To let the style encoder $E_\textrm {style}$ extract translation-invariant style information, we augment the input RGB images by four image translation methods: cropping, horizontal-flipping, rotating and scaling. we augment the input sketch images by erasing lines and thickening lines randomly. During the training stage, the translations are randomly configured and combined, then we get the features of translated RGB image $X_{rgb}^{trans}$ and the features of translated sketch image $X_{skt}^{trans}$ . To ensure features of the synthesized picture will not change compared with the original picture under different transformation methods, we use a reconstruction loss between the decoded image feature $X_{rgb}^{ae}$ and the original image feature $X_{g}^{org}$ . To strengthen the content invariant on $f_\textrm {style}$ , a triplet loss $\mathcal {L}_{tri}^{skt}$ as shown in Eq. ( 8 ) is also leveraged to encourage the cosine similarity on style feature f style to be high between the translations of the same image and to be low between different images.

\begin{align} \mathcal {L}_{tri}^{skt}=max(cos(\mathbf {f_{skt}^{t}},\mathbf {f_{skt}^{org}})-cos(\mathbf {f_{skt}^{t}},\mathbf {f_{skt}^{neg}})+\alpha,0) \end{align}
(8)
where α is the margin, $f_{skt}^{t}$ and $f_{skt}^{org}$ are feature vectors from the same image, and $f_{skt}^{neg}$ is from a different random image. It guides the encoder module to map the styles by the semantic meanings of each region, rather than the absolute pixel locations in the image.

To make the style features extracted by our encoder module more focused on colour and fine-grained texture, and to make the decoder recover the styles by memorizing those unique content-to-style relations. We utilized a mutual-information loss objective to make sure $E_\textrm {style}$ gets the most style information and decouples the style-content relation on $E_\textrm {content}$ . Specifically, a group of augmented images translated from the same image are treated as a unique class, and an auxiliary classifier is trained to classify them. To distinguish different images, $E_\textrm {style}$ is enforced to capture a more distinct style from each image. The loss function $\mathcal {L}_{cls}^{skt}$ as shown in Eq. ( 9 ) for style classification is as follows, where $E_{style}^{cls}$ is implemented as a linear layer and yields the class prediction vector and label.

\begin{align} \mathcal {L}_{cls}^{skt}=-log(\frac{exp(E_\textrm {style}^{cls}(\mathbf {f_\textrm {style}})[label])}{\sum _{j}exp(E_\textrm {style}^{cls}(\mathbf {f_\textrm {style}})[j])}) \end{align}
(9)

By minimizing the mutual-information $MI(\mathbf {f_\textrm {style}}, \mathbf {f_\textrm {content}})$ as shown in Eq. ( 10 ), the model can further decouple the correspondence between $f_\textrm {style}$ and $f_\textrm {content}$ . H refers to entropy. We consider the style information can hardly be predicted based on $f_\textrm {content}$ .

\begin{align} MI(\mathbf {f_\textrm {style}},\mathbf {f_\textrm {content}})=H(\mathbf {f_\textrm {style}})-H(\mathbf {f_\textrm {style}}\vert \mathbf {f_\textrm {content}}) \end{align}
(10)

Sketches are abstract and stroke-deformed, which requires the model to generate appropriate content from some abstract and stroke-deformed sketch. We strengthen the content feature using data augmentation such as masking out sketch randomly. We employ a triplet loss $\mathcal {L}_{cls}^{c}$ as shown in Eq. ( 11 ) to make sure all the sketch paired to the same X rgb have similar features.

\begin{align} \mathcal {L}_{cls}^{c}=max(d(\mathbf {f_{c}^{t}},\mathbf {f_{c}^{pos}})-d(\mathbf {f_{c}^{t}},\mathbf {f_{c}^{neg}})+\beta,0) \end{align}
(11)
where d(,) is the mean-squared distance, β is the margin, $f_{c}^{t}$ and $f_{c}^{pos}$ are features from the sketches that correspond to the same X rgb , and $f_{c}^{neg}$ is from a mismatched sketch. Such data augmentation could make content feature more robust to the line changes on sketches and can generate clothing pictures with reasonable semantic information and clear outlines.

Our encoders extract the disentangled features $f_\textrm {content}$ and $f_\textrm {style}$ , and the decoder D rgb takes $f_\textrm {content}$ and $f_\textrm {style}$ via channel-wise multiplication to synthesize a reconstructed image. The summed loss function $\mathcal {L}_{e}$ as shown in Eq. ( 12 ) for our encoder module is:

\begin{align} \mathcal {L}_{e}=\mathbb {E}[\left\Vert D_{rgb}(E_{s}(\mathbf {X_{rgb}},E_{c}(\mathbf {X_{skt}}))-\mathbf {X_{rgb}}\right\Vert ^{2}]+\mathcal {L}_{tri}^{skt}+\mathcal {L}_{cls}^{skt}+\mathcal {L}_{cls}^{c} \end{align}
(12)

Once our encoder module is trained, we fix it and train a GAN to revise the synthesized pictures for a better synthesis quality. Our Generator G has an encoder-decoder structure, which takes $X_{rgb}^{ae}$ from D rgb as input and generates our final results $X_{rgb}^{gan}$ . In this model, the decoder D rgb gets good style features and rich content information as shown in Fig. 6 , while the generator G enables the images to be more refined. 5 EVALUATION

We evaluate the capability of our StyleMe system in automatically generating personalized clothing sketches and style transfer from three aspects. First, we made a user study on the influence of different-quality generated sketches on sketch style transfer. 20 participants are invited in this user study to complete 9 comparison tasks to understand the participants’ preferences. Second, we made an individual user study on the performance of StyleMe by comparing the efficiency of designers with StyleMe. In the procedure of the user study, 30 participants were asked to complete a design task with and without StyleMe. Third, we made a controlled within-subject user study to evaluate the performance of StyleMe's functionality on clothing sketch generation and sketch style transfer. In the procedure of the user study, one professional team with 5 participants was recruited to asked to design 100 works based on different clothing categories, and one amateur team with 5 participants was asked to use StyleMe to generate design works according to the works designed by the professional team. Then, 100 participants were recruited to assess the quality of the generated works. Last, we made a semi-structured interview with two professional fashion designers to evaluate the usability of StyleMe. In the in-depth interview, the two experts were asked to use StyleMe to design clothing sketches with a given theme, and provide some comments on the performance of StyleMe at last. In this section, we first introduce the data preparation process for training the sketch generation model and style transfer model before experiments. Then, we introduce the study designs, report the study results, and discuss subjective feedback from study participants regarding the corresponding experiments mentioned before.

5.1 Data Preparation

Prior to the experiments, we assigned two students majored in fashion design from the design school to generate a dataset of 200 clothing-sketch pairs to train the model; the professional hand-drawn clothing sketch data were collected from one designer with 4 years of fashion design experience and one designer with 6 years of fashion design experience respectively. We also asked one designer with 4 years of design experience and one designer with 5 years of design experience to provide their clothing sketch data; they are not professional fashion designers but have an interest in learning it. In addition, we crawled 3000 clothing pictures from the clothing website to construct a fashion dataset, which was used for designers’ reference and inspiration. Designers can choose to draw sketches completely according to the existing clothing pictures, or they can only refer to these clothing pictures but not design according to the existing clothing pictures. Each designer was asked to draw 100 sketches for various clothing. Then we select 119 sketches to make the fashion designers design the clothing colour match, so as to compare with the generated clothing images after the style transfer. To ensure the performance of the deep generative model and avoid overfitting, we make dataset augmentation by four image translation methods including cropping, horizontal-flipping, rotating and scaling and two sketch translation including erasing lines and thickening lines randomly.

5.2 Experiment 1: Performance of Model Components

We examined the performance of two model components in StyleMe, which are adaptive channel feature normalization module (AdaLIN) and channel attention module (CAM), through two types of comparison tasks including sketch generation and style transfer and a follow-up user study. Considering that these two components have an important contribution to the generative adversarial network of sketch generation, we test how these components affect the quality of sketch generation by changing the network structure and then input sketches of different quality to observe whether the result of sketch style transfer is affected. Our first hypothesis was that our model without AdaLIN and CAM modules could greatly damage the effect of sketch generation. The second hypothesis was that the poor quality of the generated sketch would affect the result of sketch style transfer.

We use two quantitative metrics to evaluate the experimental performance of our model which include Frechet Inception Distance (FID) [ 17 ] and Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) [ 49 ]. FID is used to measure the overall semantic realism of the synthesized images. The smaller the value, the higher the quality of the generated image. LPIPS is used to measure the perceptual similarity between the generated image and the real image. The smaller the value, the higher the quality of the generated image. We also compare our model to other sketch-to-image synthesis and style transfer methods, including one variant of diffusion models which have attracted much attention recently as an emerging approach.

Fig. 7 shows the effect of the StyleMe system and its variants to generate sketches. The StyleMe column represents the generated sketches of our StyleMe system, the StyleMe w/o AdaLIN column represents our original methods without AdaLIN module, the StyleMe w/o CAM column represents our original methods without channel attention module, and the StyleMe w/o CAM and AdaLIN column represents our original methods without AdaLIN module and channel attention module. As shown in Fig. 7 , the sketch lines generated by StyleMe include many details, which is more consistent with the style of human fashion designers, resulting in more three-dimensional effects. The sketch lines generated by StyleMe without AdaLIN have no obvious changes, and the local details of the sketch are incomplete. The sketch lines generated by StyleMe without CAM are partially unclear. The clothing sketch generated by StyleMe w/o CAM and AdaLIN is low-quality, and the local lines are incomplete and indistinct. These experimental results have verified the first hypothesis that our model without AdaLIN and CAM modules could greatly damage the effect of sketch generation.

5.4 Experiment 3: Efficiency and Effectiveness

To evaluate the quality of the generated design works, we set up a group of experiments to evaluate whether our system has good efficiency and effectiveness in terms of professionalism, universality, and personalization in two phases.

Procedure. At last, we collected 2 design works from each professional designer, and 2 design works from each amateur designer.

In the second phase, 100 participants are recruited to conduct the experiment in an online way. 6 DISCUSSION

As suggested in our user experiment, StyleMe is designed to generate clothing sketches similar to the style of human designers in a short period of time. The style transfer function could create multiple design alternatives and ensure the diversity of fashion design. In this section, we discuss the limitations and implications of our work.

6.1 Edge Cases and Failure Modes

In the current implementation of StyleMe system, though it can achieve clothing sketch generation with comparably better results than existing works in most cases as illustrated in previous experiments, there are still some edge cases where failures may happen. Fig. 12 gives several cases where the system doesn't work, In Fig. 12 (a), the RGB image represents the original clothing image, the Hand-drawn Sketch image represents the fashion designer's draft, and the Generated Sketch represents the generated clothing sketch. In Fig. 12 (b), the Sketch image represents the generated clothing sketch, the Reference Style image represents the user-specified image, and the Generated Image represents the style-transferred clothing image.

In the sketch generation stage, when the clothing image texture is dense or a variety of colours are interlaced, the sketch texture generated by the system is a low-quality pixel block such as the first case and the third case in Fig. 12 (a). When the clothing's local details are complex and variable, the sketch generated by the system only has a basic contour such as the second case in Fig. 12 (a). These kinds of edge cases are not common. It only happens to some special clothes or low-quality clothing images. In the future, we can add focal loss or deeper network to focus on more complex local areas to solve these problems.

In the style transfer stage, when the specified reference clothing pictures include black and white, and the colors of generated clothing pictures are grey and white such as the first case in Fig. 12 (b). When the style of the image specified by the user is very different from the generated sketch, for example, assigning long sleeves as reference images to sleeveless clothes, the generated image will have redundant shadows and fuzzy texture such as in the second and third case in Fig. 12 (b). These edge cases only occur in the above-mentioned scenarios. For other situations, our StyleMe system can work well. The potential solution to fix these issues could be precisely controlling the generation of pixels through noise diffusion, such as the diffusion model.

6.2 Other Limitations

In the following part, we discuss several limitations of our system that are identified from the experiments and user feedback.

Data Preparation . In the early stage of sketch generation model, professional designers must upload one hundred of their sketch manuscripts for the model to learn the designer's fashion design style, which is an unfriendly process for designers. A potential solution is to train an advanced model which could learn the style of designers from as few designer manuscripts as possible. Besides, the input sketches must be professional-level. If the input sketch is abstract or line-deformed, it will damage the effet of clothing image generation by style transfer function. This is also a major obstacle for the tool to promote to mass designers. At the same time, for designers who have no history of manuscript upload, the generated sketches are coarse-grained, and can only generate the corresponding outlines and a few details of the designer-specified clothing. In the style transfer function, we can only input foreground clothing pictures without background patterns, and cannot input clothing pictures with other background interference. In future work, we need to improve the style transfer algorithm to reduce the limitation of style reference images.

User Interface. In the inspiration pictures and style reference pictures area of our system, we can only browse the picture library, but we can't search the clothing pictures with specified features and details. In future work, we need to embed the function of image search with sketch and text into our system. These functions can help designers find pictures quickly that can inspire design.

Function Diversity. In the automatic sketch generation function, our system can only generate sketches of complete clothing, but some designers may only need sketches of local details of clothing such as buttons and collars. How to generate local sketches of corresponding areas by marking clothing locally is a future work we need to improve. Besides, in the function of style transfer, designers may need to make modifications online in the local area of generated pictures after style transfer. If we can realize the online editing style transfer function, the system may be more attractive to designers. In addition, if our sketch generation function can pre-store several luxury-brand fashion design styles, or several Chinese typical traditional clothing styles, it will make the system more interesting.

System Access Efficiency. In the current stage, our intelligent fashion generation system only considers web access for a single designer, but fashion design sometimes requires team cooperation. How to integrate cross-device design collaboration into our system also needs to be considered. With the development of 5G technology, telecommuting has become more and more common, and the development of cross-device design collaboration is also imminent.

Evaluation Metrics. At present, the subjective evaluation index of clothing generation is based on human preference and picture authenticity. This will lead to the limitation of the evaluation criteria and the subjective influence of the individual on the evaluation score. If our evaluation index can take into account the quantifiable aesthetic factors and the objective criteria of relevant clothing colour matching, it will promote the development of intelligent fashion design industry.

6.3 Human Designer vs. AI Designer

Our evaluations showed that both professional and non-professional designers spent more time sketching and colour matching on the test than they did using StyleMe for both tasks, and that StyleMe performed comparably to human designers in terms of design quality. As suggested by both groups of study participants, their criteria for whether hand drawings were designed by humans included line tension, subject matter, detail, visual appeal, and harmony. For example, professional fashion designers pay more attention to whether the style of the generated line draft conforms to themselves, whether the details are perfect, and whether the colour matching follows the principle. However, non-professional fashion designers pay more attention to whether the theme of the line draft is clear and the composition is reasonable. According to the interviews, professional designers care more about the professionalism and personalization of intelligent generation tools, while non-professional designers care more about universality and consistency. StyleMe does both very well, as confirmed by the test of efficiency and effectiveness.

In addition, study participants were impressed by the ability of StyleMe to generate a dozen works in half a minute. They say that “ all fashion designers will be happy to use it if it saves them a lot of time in the early stages of inspiration to complete the tedious sketch of the clothing effect and the result is satisfactory. ” They also found some surprises when using StyleMe to match patterns. Style Me can combine traditional clothing patterns with colours and fabrics to create clothes that are both cultural and stylish. They also have their doubts, with some experts saying: “ If everyone can create their own clothing with their fingers, who will pay for their paintings? How to improve the professional level of designers? How to ensure the uniqueness of art? ” In fact, there is no need to worry about these problems. On the one hand, StyleMe is mainly a tool for designers to improve production efficiency. On the other hand, our tool can give designers an intuitive feeling in terms of inspiration verification, which helps them do their job better.

6.4 Generalizability

As we have seen in both the quantitative and qualitative analysis, StyleMe is favored by a majority of users in their design process. They also expressed a preference for applying StyleMe to other designs such as handbags and shoes. In fact, our approach can be generalized to other design scenarios where there are a large number of training data. For example, if there is a large number of shoe sketches and image datasets, StyleMe can also be used to generate sketches of shoes and to transfer styles from sketch to shoe image. Our model can easily transfer the styles between in-domain images. But some fashion designers want to transfer the style of grass or the tiger to the clothing sketch, which is a cross-domain image-to-image translation. Then the model needs to identify the semantic information of style and assign the pixels related to colour and texture to be reasonably distributed on the clothing sketch. Currently, the encoder module of our model cannot capture the aesthetic knowledge of complex semantics, which may not apply to complex aesthetic style transfer. To better explore more complex clothing aesthetic design, a potential solution is to embed aesthetic knowledge into the deep learning model.

7 CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK

In this paper, we present an intelligent aided fashion design system, StyleMe, for automatic personalized sketch generation and clothing sketch style transfer. To the best of our knowledge, it is the first data-driven creativity support tool for fashion design which consists of GAN-based methods to generate personalized sketches and style transfer from sketch to image according to the specific clothing image. Given a clothing image, StyleMe is able to generate personalized sketches for the designer's secondary creation or as the designer's design sketch. Meanwhile, the style transfer function can generate multiple clothing images from the sketch created by designers according to multiple images specified by the designers. The objective experimental metrics showed that the clothing images generated by our system were more real and diverse compared with other baselines. Also, the subjective user studies indicated that our system is rated higher regarding image quality as well as diversified and comprehensive functions. Besides our system achieved comparable performance compared with clothing created by human designers. Future work includes overcoming the current limitations on sketch generation and extending the system to online editing style transfer.
