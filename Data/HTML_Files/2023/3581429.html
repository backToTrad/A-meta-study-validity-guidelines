Magical Brush: A Symbol-Based Modern Chinese Painting System for Novices

Xu Haoran , College of Computer Science And Technology, Zhejiang University, China, haoranhsu@zju.edu.cn
Chen Shuyao , College of Computer Science And Technology, Zhejiang University, China, shuyaochen@zju.edu.cn
Ying Zhang , College of Fashion and Design, Donghua University, China, yingzhang@mail.dhu.edu.cn

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3581429
CHI '23: Proceedings of the 2023 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems , Hamburg, Germany, April 2023

Modern Chinese painting is a new type of painting inherited from ancient Chinese painting. Drawing modern Chinese painting is time-consuming and laborious, which is difficult for novices to start. Symbols are fundamental components of Chinese cultural works both materially and mentally. We introduce a symbol-based modern Chinese painting system termed Magical Brush. Magical Brush combines symbolic cultural factors with AI generative models, with the attempt to help novices create a complete modern Chinese painting, learn basic ideas of Chinese paintings and obtain co-creation engagement. In user study, we compare Magical Brush to other AI and non-AI digital painting tools. Results indicate that by combining cultural factors, Magical Brush can help novices easily create modern Chinese paintings and experience the cultural connotations in the process.

1 INTRODUCTION

Modern Chinese painting is a contemporary illustrative expression of traditional Chinese painting [ 51 ]. Modern Chinese painting is widely used in commercial design and is favored by many young people [ 50 ]. However, modern Chinese painting belongs to meticulous painting, with delicate lines, bright colors and complex details [ 51 ] (Figure 2 ). This makes learning and drawing modern Chinese paintings time-consuming. Research shows that novices tend to lose interest at the beginning of learning [ 42 ].

Recent advances in deep learning help novices generate aesthetic and creative visual works. For example, several deep learning models have been successfully applied to image generation [ 15 , 43 ], graphic design [ 11 , 19 ], and assisted drawing [ 29 , 38 , 39 , 54 ].

However, to the best of our knowledge, existing human-AI co-creation tools rarely support modern Chinese painting generation. In addition, existing tools rarely consider the cultural factors behind creation tasks. Cultural factors derive from domain knowledge of specific creation tasks and reflect the expected mental model in creation. In our scenario of assistance for novices, cultural factors are important. This is because the assistance system should both help novices in the painting process and allow them to experience the culture behind to build a basic mental model. To assist novices in modern Chinese painting, two challenges should be addressed:

  1. How to let novices participate in modern Chinese painting. Novices lack necessary drawing skills to finish a complete modern Chinese painting. To help them create a complete painting, an intuitive solution could be using deep generative models to generate whole images. However, in this scheme, the results are largely dominated by AI, and novices may lack a sense of participation. The challenge becomes a balance between AI assistance and human participation.
  2. How to help novices gain the knowledge of modern Chinese painting in the painting process. Novices lack the basic knowledge and cognition of modern Chinese painting. Such knowledge is important because it helps novices to form basic painting ideas and builds the necessary foundation for further exploration. The key to this challenge is to inform novices with related culture in a proper manner.

We introduce a symbol-based modern Chinese painting process for novices and thus develop a system termed Magical Brush based. Symbols are the objects of painting to express spiritual meaning, and symbols are the core of Chinese painting. Modern Chinese painting inherits symbolic culture and blends tradition with modern aesthetics. Magical Brush incorporates the cultural factor of symbols. It uses AI generative models to produce new images of symbols. To enable a wide application, the symbols learned and generated by AI models are those used frequently according to our statistics (Section 3 ). Magical Brush is expected to help novices create modern Chinese paintings easily and learn the elements of Chinese painting in the process. The contributions and the novel aspects of this work include:

  • Pattern. We propose a symbol-based pattern for human-AI collaborative painting. The symbol-based pattern is a prior from Chinese culture. There are diverse cultural priors in other creative fields. We make an attempt in human-AI collaboration by combining the cultural prior of symbols.
  • System. We develop a symbol-based modern Chinese painting system for novices. Firstly, 2,625 pictures of modern Chinese paintings by 133 illustrators are collected. Secondly, the occurrence rate and utilization rate of symbols are calculated for analysis. Thirdly, typical symbols are summarized to form the component of Magical Brush. When using the proposed system, novices can freely generate, adjust and place symbols to form their work.
  • Evaluation. Through comparative study and expert interviews, we evaluate the user experience, creativity support effects and cultural aspects of Magical Brush. And we further discuss implications for human-AI co-creative systems.

2 RELATED WORK

2.1 Human-AI Co-drawing

Human-AI Co-drawing is the process by which humans and AI collaborate to achieve a drawing goal. Related work within this domain can be subdivided into two categories according to co-drawing processes.

2.1.1 Independent Drawing of AI. Independent Drawing of AI refers to the process in which a human user provides the required input and AI generates corresponding results. For instance, Neural Turing Machine [ 18 ] mimics human's painting action sequence of graffiti with recurrent neural network (RNN), a deep learning model to learn sequential data. Similarly, Sketch-RNN system [ 20 ] further helps users to complete sketches by infering and drawing new lines based on unfinished drawings. SmartPaint [ 47 ] is an AI-assisted painting system creating cartoon scenery pictures according to user-provided doodles. Recently, Stable Diffusion [ 1 , 39 ] supports users to field excellent artworks with text prompts based on Latent Diffusion Models (LDM) [ 43 ], an advanced image generation model. Users can specify text prompts to describe the desired output and a referenced image to indicate the output's basic structure, and accordingly Stable Diffusion generates multiple new but diverse images [ 1 ].

2.1.2 Human-AI Collaborative Drawing. When cooperating with collaborative drawing systems, users can get feedbacks immediately from AI and thus make adjustments before reaching final results. For example, DialogCanvasMachine [ 7 ] is a physical interactive device. Two artists and AI will take turns to paint on the canvas with their own unique colors. Artists are capable of explaining and edit AI's contribution. The D.O.U.G system [ 45 ] involves an industrial robot and works with artist Sougwen Chung. The robot is programmed to imitate artist's painting, and then artist can react to robot's painting.

Expert-oriented digital painting tools are too complex for novices to partake in digital creative expression [ 5 ]. Therefore, novices tend to lose interest at the beginning of learning [ 42 ] and suffer from the lack of inspiration [ 13 ]. Benedetti et al. design a creativity support tool termed Painting With Bob [ 5 ] to improve novices’ creativity expressions with the tool. Novices are provided with guidance and a given picture, and by using the tool, novices are able to field diverse-style paintings with various strokes, compared to the given picture. Based on user feedback and expert grading, it is concluded that intelligent tools can improve novice's upper limits of painting. Fan et al. propose CollabDraw [ 16 ] based on Sketch-RNN to test how users and tools work together to create sketches. Participants draw eight different visual concepts in a random staggered sequence. The participant draws one stroke first, and then the agent draws the next one. The alternative drawing process proceed until the participants are satisfied with the sketch. Although the types of paintings are limited, analyses after user study show that the semantic information in sketches co-created by human and AI is as much as the information in those by human participants only. Davis et al. propose Drawing Apprentice [ 12 ], an improvised drawing agent to motivate novices’ explorations. Drawing Apprentice can analyze user inputs and respond in the shared digital canvas. Experiments on Drawing Apprentice includes informal tries, formal tries and expert interviews, and the result shows: (1) non-artists are encouraged by the system to continue creation tasks with their interests intrigued and their cognitions inspired, (2) non-artists are encouraged to continue artistic tasks in an interesting and cognitive way, (3) artists are more concerned about how the system can help in painting better and completing the task than non-artists do. Oh et al. propose DuetDraw [ 38 ] to study users’ preferences for collaborative communication methods and initiatives. They integrate Sketch-RNN into the prototype and design five comparative experimental conditions, including Lead-Detailed, Lead-Basic, Assisted-Detailed, Assisted-Basic and No-AI as a control condition. In the Lead condition, user is the leader while AI is the assistant, and the Assisted condition is the opposite. The Basic condition means that AI do not provide detailed guidance during the collaboration. The Detailed condition means that AI will provide detailed information and wait for confirmation from users before continue. Each user is asked to try the painting system under five conditions in a random order. The comparative experimental results show that users can obtain interesting user experience through interaction with AI. At the same time, they want to take initiative at every moment in the collaboration process.

2.2 Deep Generative Models

The basic AI technologies supporting Magical Brush are generative adversarial nets and colorization. In this part, we briefly review these technologies and their utilities in human-AI cooperation.

2.2.1 Generative Adversarial Nets. Generative adversarial network (GAN) [ 17 ] is a category of deep learning models to generate new samples like image, audio, text and so on. Enjoying the ability to generative real images, GAN has been utilized in image processing and computer vision tasks, such as image generation [ 15 , 26 , 27 ], image dehazing [ 14 , 30 , 55 ], and image inpainting [ 56 ]. The success on these computer vision tasks enables interactive applications in image manipulation. Interactive GAN (IGAN) [ 60 ] defines a class of image editing operations for redesigning shoes, clothes and buildings with formulated constrains to guarantee the realness of output images. Introspective adversarial networks [ 6 ] also offer this capability to perform interactive photo editing and have demonstrated their results mostly in face editing. Furthermore, GauGAN [ 40 ] translates users’ doodles into photorealistic landscapes. However, the generated results from these models are highly uncertain and uncontrollable; users can only control the output's structure rather than the specific details.

2.2.2 Conditioned Colorization. Colorization is the process to add plausible color information to monochrome photographs or to replace the color of colorful ones. Traditional colorization methods perform the whole task automatically, and conditioned deep colorization models allow users to control the colorization results with input conditions. These models can be roughly divided into three categories: exemplar-based models, text-based models, and stroke-based models.

The exemplar-based models take the color information from the references images to colorize the target image [ 22 , 28 , 53 ]. As a consequence, the colorization result depends on the example images selection. To obtain a satisfactory result, users are encouraged to choose a reference highly related to the target image in content. This class of colorization models requires the comparison of two images and thus the process is not real-time.

The text-based models employ text prompts and target image to generate specific colorization results [ 3 , 8 , 23 , 61 ]. Users control the colorization result with natural language description, so the models requires auxiliary components like attention mechanisms [ 8 ] or generated palette [ 3 ] to understand the text. The results of existing text-based models depend on their language process modules. Sometimes, detailed information contained in the text input cannot be well-recognized, like color of the hair, and the controllability is limited.

The stroke-based models take user-provided stroke as color information to generate target images [ 23 , 41 , 57 , 58 , 59 ]. Recent deep stroke-based colorization models are almost data-driven, which reduce user burden and generate visual-appealing results. The model only consider the target image and input strokes, so the process can be real-time and interactive.

3 SYMBOL-BASED MODERN CHINESE PAINTING

3.1 Chinese Symbol Definition

What is symbol? Husserl put forward the question of symbol and brought symbol into the experience relationship of symbolic structure [ 24 ]. In this case, a symbol has the dual nature of subjectivity of objects and objectification of a subject at the same time.

According to the concept of symbol in western philosophy, art is the product of symbol. This concept is similar to the symbols in Chinese classical aesthetics. The concept of the Chinese symbol is a unification of “meaning” (subjective feelings, spirits and verve) and “object” (objective beings). The form of Chinese paintings guided by such aesthetic symbols is an organized combination of objects, and the paintings convey symbolic meaning more than the objects itself. Take a Chinese idiom “bamboo in the chest” for example. The idiom means “have a definite idea in the heart". The idiom comes from the object bamboo in reality and conveys a positive belief than the bamboo itself [ 52 ]. This is similar to the English idiom “the best thing since sliced bread”. The idiom means “something is really good”. In this example, sliced bread is not only a kind of food but also a symbol of good benchmark thought by the speaker.

Chinese symbols have the characteristics of cultural accumulation [ 49 ]. Typical symbols were common objects in the beginning. Once they were noticed and used with strong appeal, their usage would be repeatedly followed and projected similar emotions. Thus, the symbols’ meanings are stabilized in the culture and gradually extended to diverse forms of art, like poem, painting, sculpture and music. In this paper, we mainly focus on symbols in modern Chinese paintings.

3.2 Chinese Symbols Selection

Due to cultural accumulation, symbols are typical in Chinese cultural work. We conduct a statistical analysis on the typical Chinese symbols [ 9 ]. The analysis is based on 2,625 pictures of typical modern Chinese painting. These pictures are manually collected from 133 most active illustrators in ZCOOL [ 35 ] and HUABAN [ 34 ], two of the largest websits to share modern Chinese paintings, to ensure that the statistical results are representative. For each symbol s , we calculate its occurrence rate, the proportion between the number of paintings having the symbol s and the total number of paintings. The occurrence rate indicates the symbol's coverage of creation scenes. The utilization rate is also calculated, as the proportion of the appearance number of the symbol s and the total number of paintings. It shows how frequently the symbol s appears in a random painting. Formally, P denotes the set of all paintings, and P ( s ) denotes the set of paintings with a symbol s . In addition, D ( s ) is the set of drawn objects of s in all paintings. Take the symbol of pear as an example. There are three paintings in P drawing pears, so P ( s ) has three paintings. The three paintings have 3, 3 and 5 pears, respectively. Therefore, D ( s ) contains 11 drawn pears. Given the above definitions, the occurrence rate O ( s ) and the utilization rate U ( s ) of the symbol s are formally defined as

\begin{equation} O(s) = \frac{| P^{(s)} |}{ | P | }, \quad U(s) = \frac{| D^{(s)} |}{ | P | } \end{equation}
(1)

Here | · | means the number of elements in a set. With typical symbols of high occurrence and utilization rate defined above, we can cover most of the creation scenes and needs. Part of our statistical results on the two rates are shown in Figure 3 . We finally select 17 symbols to build the system.

3.3 Symbol-Based Painting Process for Novices

The main challenge to design a human-AI collaborative painting process for novices lies in which kind of role novices play during the painting process. On the one hand, novices lack painting skills, and modern Chinese paintings are too complex for them to paint directly. On the other hand, in the painting process dominated by machines, novices are likely to lose their sense of participation. Hence, a novice-oriented collaborative process should comprehend the skill weaknesses of novices, secure their sense of participation and convey knowledge behind the creative work.

We design a symbol-based modern Chinese painting process oriented to novices. Briefly, novices decide symbols to paint and the AI model generate images of corresponding symbols during the process. Notice that different from most existing AI models [ 1 , 2 , 38 , 40 ], our generative models generate individual symbols only instead of a whole painting. Besides, novices can also adjust the color, alternate the style and reorganize the location of every symbol. Novices can also paint with normal brushes themselves. Such cooperation scheme is designed to keep novices in participation and get inspired without drawing complex details of symbols. Specifically, novices learn to express their ideas by using typical elements from symbol-based painting process and even explore new ideas when organizing symbol. The complete creation process is as follows:

(1) Composition: Select an appropriate background, adjust gradient option to compose the painting structure or use the brush to compose.

(2) Symbols generation: Use generative models to generate symbol images.

(3) Symbols adjustment and placement: Adjust the color, alternate the style and decide the location of all symbols.

(4) Supplemental drawing: Use the brush to add other elements or details.

4 MAGICAL BRUSH

We introduce Magical Brush, a symbol-based Modern Chinese painting system for novices. Figure 5 shows the basic interface of Magical Brush. The center part of the window is the drawing workspace, and the side drawer is on the left side to specify brush size or symbols generation. When using our system on a touch screen like Apple iPad, a right-hander user draws on the workspace with right hand and sets brushes with left hand conveniently.

4.1 Symbol Brush

Symbol Brush is the core component of Magical Brush. It generates new images of the symbol specified by the user, thus reducing repeated work. To build this component, we first identify 17 symbols with the high occurrence and utilization rate, and then manually collect image data of these symbols. Limited by the number of available collected data, the number of images for each symbol are different, ranging from 1,000 to 9,000. Technically, to avoid mode collapse of models, the data of each symbol trains only one generative model. Due to the limited amount of training images, differential augmentation [ 25 ] is used to stabilize the model training and improve generative quality. Unfortunately, in this case the generation is unconditional and the generated image is random. Specifically, we select StyleGAN2 [ 27 ] as the generator and ADA [ 25 ] as augmentation strategy. Notice that after training, the trained model generates new and distinctive images of the given symbol. Namely, the generated images are different from each other and never repeat. Such diversity provides multiple choices and inspiration to users. When painting, users call the trained models through the interface of Symbol Brush to quickly generate the required symbol images.

We design two interactive methods for the Symbol Brush. The user can click the drawing board to generate a single symbol image (precise generation in Figure 6 ) or draw a whole area where multiple symbol images will be generated (rapid generation in Figure 7 ). Before generation, the user can set the sizes and intervals of symbols to be generated. When generating multiple symbols of the same kind (for example, drawing a lotus pond), rapid creation can effectively help users reduce repetitive work.

4.2 Symbol-Image Colorization Desktop

Symbol-image colorization desktop enables users to recolor the generated symbol images based on specified color hint points. As initially generated symbol images are random, colorization allow users to control or explore colors of symbols. Symbol-image colorization desktop is designed also based on that color is an important cultural factor. Different colors of one symbol image may convey various meanings and effects [ 48 ]. For example, plain colors convey a meaning of simplicity, while bright colors mean vitality. Colorization allows users to harmonize and unify colors of symbols based on the main idea to express. Specifically, we use iColoriT [ 57 ] and its official pre-trained model. The colorization module is interactive and based on hint points. The colorization of a symbol images takes three steps. Firstly, the user selects a generated symbol image from the paint canvas and the selected symbol image will appear in the colorization drawer on the left side. Secondly, the user picks colors from the color-picker and clicks the symbol image in the color board to set hints. In case of misoperation, the user can rollback or reset the colorization results. Thirdly, the user applies the colorization results back to the main images in the workspace.

4.3 Symbol-Image Alternative Desktop

We set up a Symbol-Image Alternative Desktop to replace generated symbols (Figure 9 ). The generative model used in the system is unconditional, so users cannot control the generated image of a specific symbol. To alleviate this issue of uncontrollability, Symbol-Image Alternative Desktop provides a convenient way to replace the generated symbols with alternatives. Each time the user calls the model to generate a symbol image, we provide a group of images of the same class as candidates in Symbol-Image Alternative Desktop. When unsatisfied with the generated symbol image or exploring various possibilities, the user can choose a candidate image to substitute the original one.

4.4 Painting Guidance

We provide Painting Guidance for novices to get a better knowledge of modern Chinese painting. The guidance mainly consists of two types, recommendation of related symbols (Figure 10 ) and instruction for colorization (Figure 11 ). The recommendation of related symbols offers suggestions for further symbol selections based on the current canvas. The recommended symbols are closely related to the painted symbols; they have similar meanings or often form paintings of certain themes together. The instruction for colorization explains potential colors of symbols and the corresponding meanings. Take the symbol “lotus” as an example. When a lotus is generated, Painting Guidance suggests “lotus leaf” as a related symbol. When the user attempts to recolor the lotus, Painting Guidance gives a hint that other colors like white, yellow, red and dark blue can be potential choice. The meaning of lotus varies in different colors. For example, white lotus means nostalgia, while red lotus means perseverance and bravery. With Painting Guidance, novices are expected to get a deeper experience of the culture behind modern Chinese painting.

4.5 Basic Painting Tools

We also provide basic painting tools like Brush, Layer and Background Desktop. The Line Tool is for overall layout painting, element connection and detail supplement. Modern Chinese painting is a kind of illustration, and the elements basically have a relatively obvious outline. To this end, we provide the contour brush. Using the Layer Tool, the user can adjust position, size, direction and depth of a symbol image. In Background Desktop, we provide users with classic rice paper and gradient color background option widely used in modern Chinese paintings.

5 STUDY DESIGN

Magical Brush is expected to help novices create modern Chinese paintings easily and learn the elements of Chinese painting in the process. In order to verify our design intention, we conduct a comparative user study on novices with Procreate [ 33 ] and Stable Diffusion [ 1 ]. Besides, an expert evaluation is conducted focusing on the cultural and educational aspects, automation-expression balance and future improvements of Magical Brush.

5.1 Novice Study

A comparative user study is conducted on novices with Procreate and Stable Diffusion. Procreate is a representative non-AI digital painting tool with various stroke styles. With Procreate, users can try at will, but the quality of the painting largely depends on their painting skills. Stable Diffusion is an AI-based text-to-image generation system. Users input a text prompt to describe the picture they desire. The system will generate high-quality pictures accordingly. The prompts can include the expected picture content and styles with proper nouns or artists, such as “by Vincent van Gogh”.

5.1.1 Participants. Sixty participants complete the study, including 32 males and 28 females, aged between 18 and 30 (M: Mean = 22.53, SD = 1.02, F: Mean = 22.25, SD = 2.08). To ensure that participants are new to painting, we require participants to be users who have not systematically learned any painting skill within a long period of time but are interested in painting. Almost all users draw at low frequency, only occasionally or not at all.

The participants are divided into three groups and they separately try a different tool: (1) Magical Brush (10 males and 10 females, denoted as M1-M20), (2) Procreate (11 males and 9 females, denoted as P1-P20), and (3) Stable Diffusion (11 males and 9 females, denoted as S1-S20).

The participants are recruited through campus forums and websites from various professional backgrounds (such as Chinese Language, Biomedical Engineering). Every participant receives 50 CNY as labor remuneration.

5.1.2 Self-Rating Questionnaire. According to USE questionnaire [ 36 ] and Creative Support Index (CSI) [ 10 ], we design a questionnaire to quantitatively evaluate Magical Brush, Procreate, and Stable Diffusion using 11 indicators, including (1) Usefulness [ 36 ], (2) Ease of Use [ 36 ], (3) Ease of Learning [ 36 ], (4) Enjoyment [ 10 ], (5) Exploration [ 10 ], (6) Expressiveness [ 10 ], (7) Result-Worth-Effort [ 10 ], (8) Education, (9) Predictability, (10) Controllability, and (11) Satisfaction (of the created painting). Indicators 1-3 and 8-10 are scored on the 7-point Likert scale (where 1 = strongly agree, 7 = strongly disagree). Indicators 4-7 are scored on the 11-point scale (where 0 = strongly disagree, 10 = strongly agree). Indicator 11 is scored on the 100-point scale. Each indicator has 1-3 detailed items. Indicators 1-3 measure user experience of novices to complete modern Chinese paintings. Indicators 4-7 measure creativity support effect. Indicator 8 measures educational aspect of the tool. Indicators 9-10 reflect the issue pointed out in the AI interface [ 21 , 44 , 46 ]. Indicator 11 reflects user’ satisfaction with their created paintings.

5.1.3 Semi-Structured Interview. We also conduct 15-minute semi-structured interviews with audio recording. The participants are asked to describe their painting processes and painting results. Specifically, some questions are based on our observational scripts (Table 1 ), and some are based on their answers to questionnaires about their experience when using the painting tools.

5.1.4 Experimental Procedure. Participants will first complete a questionnaire about their basic personal information and initial impressions of the AI co-creation system (5 minutes). After that, they will watch the demonstration video explaining the basic function of the digital painting tool to use. Then participants are required to finish a modern Chinese painting without time restriction. During the painting process, they can search for examples on the Internet if they want. Finally, they will answer a post-study questionnaire (5 minutes) and participate in a semi-structured interview (15 minutes).

5.2 Expert Study

In expert study, we recruit two experts. They are a design faculty from a prestigious university (E1) and a working art editor in media industry (E2). We invite two experts in synchronous sessions and help them learn to use Magical Brush. After they have clear understandings of Magical Brush, we take expert interviews with audio recording mainly probing three topics (Table 2 ). (1) The topic on culture is about whether Magical Brush integrates adequate cultural factors for novices to learn and experience. (2) Balance is about their views of the balance between automation and expression in the paintings for novice users. (3) Improvement is about how can Magical Brush further assist novices and play an active role in education and cultural communication in the future.

5.3 Analysis Methods

From the above experimental design, we get two types of data. We obtain quantitative data from the questionnaire of novice comparative study, and qualitative data from novice interviews and expert interviews. We conduct the following analysis process for the quantitative data and qualitative data.

5.3.1 Quantitative Analysis. In quantitative analysis, we aim to find out if there is a significant difference between novice users’ evaluation of Magical Brush and other comparative tools for each indicator. As every sample is independent, we perform independent t-tests for each indicator, comparing the effect of each painting tool on the user experience for novices.

5.3.2 Qualitative Analysis. We transcribe the novices and experts interview records to get our qualitative results using grounded theory techniques [ 4 ]. First, we review the transcriptions and discuss main issues from observations and interviews. Second, keyword tagging and theme building are conducted using ATLAS.ti [ 37 ], a qualitative research software tool provided by Scientific Software Development GmbH. We segment transcripts into small sentences, and then use keywords to summarize them into 424 keyword tags. Finally, we refine those keywords and link those themes to form our qualitative analysis. After combining keywords by correlation, we yield 21 themes and integrate them into 6 main categories.

6.2 Qualitative Results

6.2.1 Cultural and Educational. Magical Brush mainly focuses on assistance in Modern Chinese painting. In this session, we discuss whether the tool can help novices understand the basic elements and cultural connotations of modern Chinese paintings. Through the pre-study questionnaire, we investigate users’ understandings of modern Chinese painting. Among them, 39 of 60 users only have the basic aesthetic ability, while 32 of 60 users do not have a cognition of modern Chinese painting. This indicates the necessity to provide educational assistance to novices.

With comparative study, we find that the educational effects of modern Chinese paintings provided by Stable Diffusion and Procreate are limited. Most Stable Diffusion or Procreate users report that their understanding and learning of modern Chinese paintings comes entirely from the Internet. S13 says, “Only when I have mastered the relevant knowledge of modern Chinese painting, can I know how to set the text prompts to generate what I want.” P3 says, “Procreate is a general painting software, which has no direct relationship with specific styles such as modern Chinese paintings.” Meanwhile, although Stable Diffusion participants give numerous prompts trying to specify the style to modern Chinese, such as “modern Chinese style painter”, the generative results are still far away from modern Chinese paintings. Still, some are similar to traditional Chinese painting (S8) or traditional Japanese style (S10). A few paintings that conform to the idea of modern Chinese paintings suffer from the issue of “being structurally simple” (S6).

Users trying Magical Brush basically agree that the tool can help them understand more about modern Chinese paintings. M5 states that “Magical Brush provides typical modern Chinese painting elements, so that novices need not draw the details of single objects.” We find that only 3 out of 20 Magical Brush users have opened the search engine during the painting process, hoping to get more guidance. After painting, most Magical Brush users can have a clear grasp of the style characteristics of modern Chinese paintings, such as “retain the elements of the classic Chinese paintings” (M5), “partially realistic”(M7), “more bright colors”(M10), or think that their aesthetic ability has been improved (M17).

Magical Brush users also recognize that symbols provided by Magical Brush are typical. As mentioned by M13, the symbols generated by Symbol Brush, such as “crane”, “moon” and “auspicious cloud”, are very common in traditional Chinese poetry and traditional Chinese painting. They are inherited from traditional culture and are easy to understand. And E1 and E2 also approve that symbols provided by Magical Brush is very typical in modern Chinese illustrations.

In order to further help novice experience cultural factors, Magical Brush provides Painting Guidance. During the painting, 15 out of 20 Magical Brush users try this function. Among them, 11 of 15 users state that the recommendation of related symbols gives them effective guidance and inspires them to draw matched symbols, such as drawing “clouds and cranes” (M7), “swallows and willows” (M14) in a picture. E2 approves that Painting Guidance can offer tips on the cultural connotation for novices. However, users who turn off the recommendation state that the recommendations differ from their own ideas (M19), and they prefer their own dominance of the canvas (M12). M5 also mentions that this kind of knowledge popularization method is very innovative, which helps him to practice directly in the painting process, but “the description of some words is slightly professional, and there are still some reading difficulties for novices.”

6.2.2 Controllability. Controllability measures to what extent can participants make the painting result meet their expectations with adjustments.

We find that Stable Diffusion users generally believe that the tool is not quite controllable and predictable. They state that the results are usually unexpected and are difficult to adjust through text prompts to make a detailed control of the canvas. S8 points out that although the overall color and composition of the picture can be generally controlled, more specific details like colors and strokes are still uncontrollable. S2 also points out that although the random seed has been fixed, the painting results generated by the same prompts are also different, which makes him confused about how to understand and control the results in an iterative manner. Some users also mention that they always need to guess the intention and logic of the tool to deduce their own description (S2,13).

The better controllability of Magical Brush is exemplified by that participants can make relatively detailed adjustments or replacements of symbols and determine their position exactly. M2 states that “through deletion and recall, I can make the painting results within a satisfactory range”. M19 also mentions that the colorization module can control the material to “have different colors”, which is her most satisfying function.

6.2.3 Participation and Expression. There are obvious differences in user participation between Magical Brush and the comparative tools. In this session, we study the preferences of novice users to tools with different participation.

When painting with Stable Diffusion, the users’ participation is limited to text prompts writing and some basic model settings like results resolutions and random seeds. The Stable Diffusion participants generally admit that AI plays a leading role when painting. “AI has the final rights of the work.” (S13) However, many participants believe that the sense of participation and expression is low. “Stable Diffusion can indeed provide a sense of freshness in a short time, but I think the sense of participation and experience of novices is more important.” (S11) “In fact, this is just a reproduction of others’ style, but not my own characteristics.” (S19) At the same time, some users only care whether the results provided by the AI are good enough (S6, S14).

On the other hand, Procreate is generally believed to have a full sense of participation. A large number of rich strokes expand their exploration space and freedom, allowing users to express ideas freely. When we ask Procreate users whether they would like to improve AI participation in the tool, some users agree with this opinion. They think it will be “very friendly to novices” (P20) and “can help optimize lines” (P10). Whereas, some users hold a more cautious attitude. “It depends on whether the user's purpose is to improve the quality of painting or to ensure full free expression.” (P18)

From the comparison of the two tools above, although novice users have weak painting skills, their exploration wills and expression awareness are still very strong. This indicates that we need to find a balance between AI assistance and human expression. Magic Brush can reduce the difficulty of painting and guarantee painting quality to some degree. M17 mentions that “Procreate is suitable for professional painters, while Stable Diffusion features strong entertainment. The participation of the Magical Brush is in the middle of the two. It is suitable for novices who have no skills but desire to create.” Most Magical Brush users think that they play a fairly dominant role in the painting process. M2 states that the participation of AI is mainly to generate random elements, and the rest of the painting process is remained to be done by users. M10 points out that compared with Stable Diffusion, the painting process of Magical Brush is more visible, providing room for users to adjust details. Some users also argue that to make the co-creation attribute of AI and users stronger, AI should continue to optimize on the basis of the paintings by users, such as “adding background and coordinating pictures” (M3).

On the balance between automation and expression, E2 believes that the balance is important for novices. E2 states that “The created work by Stable Diffusion is not a result by human but by machine. Even if a child plays toy bricks, there are personal imaginations and emotions. The format of Magical Brush is similar to advanced collages, and collages also have personal feelings in it. The significance of Magical Brush is the process in which users can use generated elements as semi-automation while they are still responsible for conceiving the whole painting. AI only helps users to express the desired symbols so users keep a sense of participation.” And E1 states that it is necessary to further subdivide the users into groups when talking about this issue; “novices can be further divided into those who emphasize painting experience and those who attach importance to painting results”.

6.2.4 Efficiency. Through the comparison of the painting process and the main efforts of users, we explore the impact of the three tools on users’ painting efficiency.

We find that in the process of using Stable Diffusion, the main part of users’ efforts is to explore what description can be “understood” by AI. They find that the generation results of living creatures like shrimps and foxes are very strange (S5, S6), and the generation of faces is usually not harmonious (S8). Hence, they have to adjust the content, trying to find objects that AI can produce with a better quality. Although the qualifiers related to modern Chinese paintings are given, the demanded objects still do not appear (S13). This increases users’ confusion and reduces their efficiency. S19 points out that he spends a lot of effort on trial and error to achieve relatively satisfactory results.

Procreate users generally use a more conventional painting process. Namely, participants first draw lines, then pave the background color, color the object, and finally stroke the edge. P14 points out that this is a conventional painting process, so it cannot help him improve his efficiency. Due to the need for hand painting, the average painting time of Procreate users is 1.5-2 times that of the other two groups. Most Procreate users point out that rich strokes simplify the difficulty of detailed drawing and can improve some efficiency for users. But for novices, “it still takes a lot of time to learn to use these strokes skillfully”(P12).

Magical Brush users generally first select a background, do basic composition, then generate and adjust symbols, and supplement some details with the brush. As mentioned by M13, after selecting the background, he first combines the mountains and pavilions to form an occlusion relationship. Then he adds the elements of auspicious clouds and trees recommended by Painting Guidance and adjusts their sizes. Symbol generation saves time for users to draw details, which improves efficiency. However, it still takes time to select generated symbol images that meet user preferences. Users usually generate a large number of symbols, and reserve the appropriate ones (M4). Or they use Symbol-Image Alternative Desktop to replace them one by one. However, it is generally accepted by users that the time spent on selection could be much less than that spent on hand painting.

6.2.5 Satisfaction. Through the study, we find that the most satisfied and dissatisfied functions of users basically revolve around the core functions of each tool. For Stable Diffusion, users are generally satisfied with its ease of use, efficient output and high picture completion. S10 states that Stable Diffusion can be used to quickly produce paintings imaginary and novel. Stable Diffusion users believe that the limitations of the tool mainly lie in the poor performance of the generation of some specific styles like modern Chinese painting (S8) and the weak controllability of the results.

For Procreate, we find that users are generally satisfied with the rich brush strokes, but they think that the excessive choices of brushes are not friendly to novices in short-term use. P7 points out that Procreate gives users various options, but should offer novices some recommendations and guidance. P17 also states that novice users could not remember which brush they have used before, which causes inconvenience. We also found Procreate users’ evaluation of toolbars such as layers and colors polarized. Some users think that the coverage relationship of the layers is unclear (P15), while others think that the layers are clear and easy to arrange (P8). Some users think that the color disk can facilitate them to select the desired color (P9), while others think that color adjustment is quite difficult (P13). From these feedbacks, Procreate is not suitable for novices to use in the short term as a professional tool due to its complexity and the lack of guidance.

For Magical Brush, the most satisfactory function for users is symbol generation, which they think is very friendly to novices. Most novice painters lack basic skills to grasp the specific details of symbols and their color matching, so it is difficult for them to draw relatively fine-grained works. M10 points out that “symbol generation eliminates the complicated steps of line drawing and coloring”. M6 also believes that the painting quality lower limit of Magical Brush is high, which gives novice users a stronger sense of achievement with their participation and efforts. However, what participants are not satisfied with is that the styles of some images are not uniform (M12), and that the types of images are limited (M13). Some users point out that although Magical Brush raises the painting's lower bound, it also lowers the upper bound; it cannot let professional painters play to a high level. For novice users, this is not interference.

6.2.6 Predictability, Inspiration and Exploration. Through the pre-study questionnaire, we find that 42 out of 60 participants admit that the reason for low painting frequency is a lack of inspiration.

For Procreate, some users think that its inspiration comes from its various strokes. P2 points out, “When I see the brush stroke of water drops, I will think about whether this effect can be combined with the water surface to create a realistic lake. This inspired my desire to explore.”

The participants also generally recognize the inspiration brought by Stable Diffusion. The generated works of Stable Diffusion are of greater randomness, and the unexpected results will broaden the thinking of users. S4 points out that even if the text prompts are modified just a little, the results will be quite different, which provides him with new ideas. S10 also mentions that Stable Diffusion is very suitable for obtain concrete and inspiring results by inputting wild ideas. However, some users believe that on the other hand, the various and unpredictable output of Stable Diffusion limits an iterative exploration of users’ current thoughts. “If I find that the generated results are always strange after I tried a keyword, I can only give up my current exploration.” (S7)

Magical Brush users generally agree that Magical Brush is enlightening for novice creation. After obtaining inspiration, the users can also implement their ideas in the creative process with clear understandings of the creation logic of Magical Brush. The inspiration of Magical Brush comes from the diversity of generated symbol images. Take lotus for example, after drawing a whole area with Symbol Brush, it will randomly generate various lotus flowers with different shapes, colors and open states. This increases the diversity of paintings and helps novices observe different drawings of lotus flowers with just one stroke. M10 mentions that “various pavilion elements provide me with a large but unique material library. If I want to draw freely, these elements can also give me more ideas.” On the other hand, users have free trials freely by matching and adjusting symbols until they reach a harmonious canvas. M13 points out that each mountain can move independently and combine freely, adding to his sense of achievement in organizing his own pictures.

6.2.7 Limitations and Improvements. When cooperating with three tools, users’ participation varies. And different co-creation formats have their own limitations. In this session, we summarize the limitations of the tools and explore the future improvement of Magical Brush.

We find that two AI tools constrict users’ personal characteristics and ideas to some extent. Namely, they may not help novices improve their painting skills. Stable Diffusion is an efficient and interesting auxiliary creation tool. The production quality can be relatively high, far exceeding the actual painting level of novices. However, as the user mentioned, “the creativity of the picture comes entirely from Stable Diffusion” (S5), and “exquisite paintings lack human emotional expression” (S7). The similar problem exists with Magical Brush. The options of symbols that can be combined and matched are relatively limited. While providing assistance with Painting Guidance, Magical Brush may also “limit users to fixation” (M4, E2). In contrast, users generally admit the non-AI tool Procreate is a tool with a high upper bound, and the largest limitations lie in novice users themselves. They cannot skillfully and effectively control the brush and composition. P15 says, “I can only draw general outlines, but cannot control details.” Most users hope that AI can be introduced to further assist them, for example in shape details (P2) and drawing sketches (P4).

To improve Magical Brush, E1 suggests that AI can be applied in harmonizing the paintings, and the results can be animated to emphasize the atmosphere of Chinese paintings. Other suggestions for Magical Brush are to improve the novice user experience by “adding mirror flip or transparency adjustment options” (M8) and “assisting in composition” (M7).

7 DISCUSSION

7.1 Users’ Initiative

Through user questionnaires and qualitative study, we do not observe users determine the indicator Satisfaction according to the painting's visual quality. Many Procreate participants rate high in Satisfaction (of the created painting) even if they are not proficient in the tool and lack painting skills. Similarly, many users reject the creative alternative of Stable Diffusion and rate low in Satisfaction (of the created painting) since AI dominates the results. This discovery can be partially explained by the finding “let the user take the initiative” in the previous study [ 38 ].

On the other hand, Magical Brush explores a proper balance between AI automation and users’ initiative. Magical Brush assigns the part of detailed elements drawing to AI, which is novice users incapable of, and leaves the part of element arrangement to human. As a result, Magical Brush gets significantly higher rates than the other two tools in the indicator Satisfaction (of the created work). To some extent, this implies that user's own ability should be considered and emphasized in designing human-computer collaboration systems.

7.2 Implementing the Inspired Ideas

We find that the randomness of AI is the source of inspiration for novice users. The user study of Drawing Apprentice [ 12 ] has proved that novices are more willing to explore than professional painters. They value inspiration and need to be motivated more to raise their painting upper limits. From Stable Diffusion to Magical Brush, AI-involved tools not only introduce stochasticity, but also bring users a fresh perspective of thinking because of the difference between the generative results and their predictions.

However, it is also important to help users realize creative ideas after inspirations. We find that although Stable Diffusion can help novices form creative ideas, due to the obstacles in understanding the tool's generation logic, users will be affected by uncontrollable results when exploring new ideas. In this case, even given the inspired idea from unpredictability, they struggle to realize the idea due to uncontrollability and thus discard the simulated inspiration.

Previous study reveals users’ strong preference for manual rectification after automation [ 44 ]. With symbols adjustments enabled, quantitative analysis shows that compared with Stable Diffusion, Magical Brush is more explored and has significantly higher controllability, which helps users accomplish their goals as expected after inspired.

7.3 Integrating Cultural Factors

The symbol is a prior knowledge of culture intrinsically. We examine the prior assumption of symbols based on our statistical analysis of our illustrations dataset and user study. The assumption is that symbols are classical and typical. We extract cultural symbols from modern Chinese paintings to build Magical Brush. Magical Brush effectively helps novices create modern Chinese paintings, consistent with the findings in previous study that AI tools help in breaking the co-creation task down into understandable chunks for novices [ 31 ].

The insight behind Magical Brush will not be limited to modern Chinese paintings. The concepts similar to symbols can also be followed in other painting types, such as surrealism, deconstructionism and Art Deco. Our research explores the idea to integrate cultural prior in Human-AI co-creation in cultural fields.

8 CONCLUSION

We introduce Magical Brush, a symbol-based modern Chinese painting system for novices. User study shows Magical Brush can help novices with modern Chinese painting in various indicators. The result exemplifies the effectiveness of integrating cultural prior. However, Magical Brush still needs further improvements. For example, it can reduce the interference and optimize the form of expression when it comes to the cultural and educational aspects of AI. Lastly, we hope this work can shed light on co-creation between novices and AI from the perspective that AI acts as a carrier of culture.
