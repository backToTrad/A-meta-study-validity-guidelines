Existing situated visualization models assume the user is able to directly interact with the objects and spaces to which the data refers (known as physical referents). We review a growing body of work exploring scenarios where the user interacts with a proxy representation of the physical referent rather than immediately with the object itself. This introduces a complex mixture of immediate situatedness and proxies of situatedness that goes beyond the expressiveness of current models. We propose an extended model of situated visualization that encompasses Immediate Situated Visualization and ProxSituated (Proxy of Situated) Visualization. Our model describes a set of key entities involved in proxSituated scenarios and important relationships between them. From this model, we derive design dimensions and apply them to existing situated visualization work. The resulting design space allows us to describe and evaluate existing scenarios, as well as to creatively generate new conceptual scenarios.

1 INTRODUCTION

Situated visualization puts forward the idea that, in certain tasks, placing data visualization into the physical environment from which the data was collected can benefit the user's contextual understanding of the data. Thomas et al. [ 90 ] define situatedness in a situated analytics scenario as being the degree to which the task involves a connection between the person, the data, and physical objects in the environment. An early (2006) example of such situated visualization was given by White et al. [ 96 ], in their virtual voucher augmented reality system, designed to aid botanists in visualizing plant data during fieldwork. Following that proof of concept is a large body of data visualization work that integrates visual representations of data into the physical context of objects and environments (i.e., the physical referents); often using Augmented Reality (AR) technology.

More recently, there is a growing body of work extending the idea of situated visualization by exploring scenarios where the user interacts with a proxy representation of the physical referent rather than the immediate object or environment. Examples of such proxies in situated visualization include immersive virtual environments (e.g., [ 72 ]) or physical scale models (e.g., [ 27 ]). Proxy representations provide a new type of situatedness, which we call proxsituatedness . We define proxsituatedness as the degree to which a task involves a connection between the person, the data, and the physical referent proxy. We propose the term ProxSituated Visualization to describe this class of work. Proxy representations of physical referents ease spatial and temporal constraints of traditional situated visualization, allowing for a wider range of scenarios than situated visualization at the immediate location. Technologies to digitalize physical referents and to perceive their representations (e.g., virtual reality, augmented reality, and 3D printing) have enabled the integration of proxies of the physical referents into the data visualization workflow at a relatively low cost. However, achieving immediate situatedness and proxsituatedness in situated visualization comes with different design and technical requirements. A distinction between the two approaches is crucial for developing situated visualization research and practice, but it is not currently defined.

Existing situated visualization models describe the relationships between data, visualization pipeline, data representation, physical referent, and the user [ 32 , 90 , 97 , 100 ] (Section 2 ). They emphasize the integration between visualizations and immediate physical referents. Willett et al. [ 100 ] were the first to suggest using a facsimile as a substitute for an immediate physical referent. Yet, current models cannot expressively characterize situated visualization involving proxsituatedness. As an illustration, imagine a scenario where a remote analyst works with digital models of machines while a local analyst in the factory has direct access to the machines themselves. Current models are too general to differentiate the immediate situatedness created by actual machines and their environment, with proxsituatedness achieved by proxy representations of the machines experienced by the remote analyst. Proxsituatedness introduces more visualization design possibilities and challenges with blended physical and digital representations and other factors, such as trade-offs between static tangible proxies and dynamic digital imagery. To properly discuss and consider such scenarios, we need a more nuanced situated visualization model than those previously proposed. We elaborate on this further in Sections 2 and 3.

Apart from the limitations of current models, current literature also lacks formal terminology for such situated visualizations. For instance, a recent review on situated visualization [ 7 ] contains a notable number of examples using proxy representations. However, without a more nuanced set of definitions, it is not easy to articulate scenarios involving situated visualization performed in the immediate environment of the data and situated visualization using proxy representations of that environment. The limitation of current situated visualization models and the unavailability of terminology for situated visualization using proxsituatedness leaves a gap in our ability to precisely describe or compare a variety of scenarios.

To address this gap, this paper proposes an extended model of situated visualization that explicitly considers both immediate situatedness and proxsituatedness (Section 3 ). To achieve this, our model introduces new entities for environment , physical referent proxy , and environment proxy . Our model also characterizes relationships between these entities using spatial indirection , temporal indirection , representation scale , and representation form . We provide an example scenario illustrating how our model fits into immediate situated and proxsituated visualization.

Using the proposed entities and relationships, we formed a design space and explored existing proxsituated visualization work to understand the literature landscape better and identify potential future work (Section 4 ). Through a literature review, we have learned that our model can not only describe existing work but also precisely compare, evaluate, and generate designs for proxsituated visualization. We found that existing proxsituated visualization work predominantly creates proxsituatedness by arranging referent and environment representations similar to the immediate situation (e.g., scale models on a map, 3D digital models in a virtual environment), and that visualization of historical data is the most common scenario. Downscaling large physical referents, aggregating data values, and employing digital proxy representations are also apparent trends. Based on our findings, we propose several future directions, including but not limited to combining immediate and proxsituatedness, designing live representations, as well as “proxsituating” data physicalization (Section 5 ). Then, an acknowledgment of a limitation (Section 6 ) and a concluding remark (Section 7 ) wrap up our paper.

2 SITUATED VISUALIZATION

This section provides a historical context and existing models of situated visualization, as well as describes the key motivation for the introduction of an extended model.

2.1 Historical context

Seminal work in situated visualization was presented by White et al. [ 95 , 96 , 97 ]. In 2006, they introduced situated visualization as “ ... a visualization that is located and overlaid in a place where it is meaningful and where the place adds to the visualization ” [ 96 , p. 7]. Then in 2009, motivated by urban planning site visits, White and Feiner [ 95 ] introduced a well-cited work of situated visualization called SiteLens. Over the last decade, several definitions of situated visualization have emerged. This includes situated analytics, which was introduced by Elsayed et al. [ 23 ] in 2015 and was further described in a book chapter by Thomas et al. [ 90 ]. Situated analytics is differentiated from other situated visualization approaches by its interactive analytical features [ 23 , 32 ]. In 2016, Willett et al. [ 100 ] introduced an influential model of situated visualization; we will revisit their work in the subsequent section.

A recent review by Bressa et al. [ 7 ] revealed a large body of work in situated visualization, including several situatedness definitions [ 8 , 21 , 63 , 79 , 89 ]. They found that these definitions mainly revolve around data representation in the proximity of physical features of the environment and, to a limited extent, a broader definition encompassing contextual, social, and local situatedness factors [ 57 ]. In recent years, variations of situated visualization topics, including situated visualization for moving objects [ 102 ], for sports [ 48 , 50 ], or video games [ 9 ], have emerged. We anticipate that more genres of situated visualization will take form in the near future. We specifically look into the type of situated visualization that uses proxies of physical referents as this type of work has numerous design possibilities, but its conceptual model is under-explored.

2.2 Existing models

We review existing situated visualization work by categorizing them into the following: 1) the initial model that was first introduced, 2) the extended model, and 3) other models that build on existing models.

The initial model by White [ 97 ], 2009 . In their thesis, “ Interaction and presentation techniques for situated visualization ”, White described three characteristics of situated visualization: 1) data related to physical context, 2) visualization that is relevant to the physical context, and 3) display and presentation of the visualization in the physical context. Building from these characteristics, they define a design space consisting of context (objects or spaces related to the data), relevance (relationship between the data and context), locus of presentation, and locus of interaction.

The extended model by Willett et al. [ 100 ], 2016 . Willett et al.’ model is currently the most cited situated visualization model. We highlight three key foundations presented by their work. Firstly, they introduced the term physical referent , which has a similar meaning to the physical context in White's model [ 97 ], i.e., objects or spaces to which the data refers. Second, the Willett et al.’ model describes the relationship between data representation medium (visualization versus physicalization [ 42 ]) and integration of data representation and referent, from which they proposed the term embedded representation . Embedded data representation is a subset of situated representation that has very close physical integration between data representation and physical referent (refer to Fig. 4 in Willett et al. [ 100 ] for reference). For simplicity of our proposed model, we do not use the term representation to differentiate between visualization and physicalization. Instead, we use the term visualization throughout the paper. In the Conclusion section, we describe how the term “representation” can be adapted to our model.

The third key point is the entities and relationships in their model. According to Willett et al. [ 100 ], spatially situated visualization involves four key entities: 1) raw data, 2) visualization pipeline, 3) data representation 1 , and 4) physical referent. Raw data and the visualization pipeline do not have a physical representation and, thus, are located in the logical world . Whereas physical referent and data representation are located in the physical world . More on this model is described later.

Other extended models . A few extended models have been introduced, including a model of situated analytics by ElSayed et al. [ 22 ], which was considered as situated visualization that focuses on supporting interactive analytical tasks [ 22 , 25 ]. A model by Thomas et al. [ 90 ] adapts Willett et al.’ model and situated analytics concept by considering user perception and interaction. Martin et al.[ 59 ] incorporate situatedness perspectives [ 7 ] into Thomas et al.’ model. Recently, Fleck et al. [ 32 ] proposed a model that contrasts immersive analytics and situated analytics. We took a step back and designed a model of proxsituated visualization based on Willett et al.’ model [ 100 ] as these existing models do not clearly capture situated visualizations with proxy representations.

2.3 Why do we need an extended model?

In the introduction, we mention factory data analysis scenarios that illustrate where the current model fails to conceptualize proxsituated visualizations. This section elaborates further by describing three key points that strongly motivate the introduction of an extended model.

An observation that existing situated visualizations use proxy representations of physical referent and environment. Salient works that review situated visualization, such as those of Willett et al. [ 100 ] and Bressa et al. [ 7 ], place emphasis on data visualization at immediate physical locations. Several papers they cited employed both digital and physical representations of physical referents and, to some extent, referred to their work as situated visualization. Examples include but are not limited to Uplift [ 27 ], Corsican Twin [ 72 ], Simultaneous Worlds [ 39 ], and immersive situated bar graph [ 74 ]. While we agree that those contemporary works fit into situated visualization models, we assert that a precise classification of techniques that is capable of separating immediate and proxsituatedness will benefit the visualization research field moving forward as the two approaches have distinctive design and technical requirements. For instance, tools for immediate situated visualization (e.g.,  [ 10 , 32 , 65 ]) require registrations of virtual charts in the physical environment, whereas proxsituated visualization tools might not have such constraints.

Current literature lacks formal terminology or conceptual models for these types of work. We encountered uses of the term “proxy” around situated visualization and mixed-reality research. Prozeau et al. [ 72 ] stated that digital twins could be used as a “ proxy for authoring AR applications ”. Mathis et al. [ 61 ] mentioned the use of XR as a “ proxy for real-world research ”. Another term, facsimile, was also discussed by Willett et al. [ 100 ] as a possible physical referent proxy in situated visualization. Despite that, the term proxy has not been defined further, and the characteristic of situated visualization with proxy representation is under-explored. This leaves unanswered questions such as: How can we describe situated visualizations that are not performed in the immediate location? What entities should we consider? As mixed-reality technology improves, it is going to become more difficult for users to distinguish reality and virtuality [ 62 , 82 ]. Hence, it is important to have precise definitions so that we know what we are communicating and can describe what we are looking at.

Broad possible design space with promising opportunities. Technologies such as digital twin, asynchronous reality (e.g., [ 30 ]), digital avatar (e.g., [ 71 ]), immersive macroscopic view (e.g., [ 84 ]), and additive manufacturing [ 101 ], to name a few, are capable of creating a rich proxy representation of physical referents and environments. Combining these technologies with data representation techniques such as immersive visualization, data physicalization [ 42 ], or both can create a vast design space, but it has not been thoroughly synthesized. Our paper aims to lay a foundation for future exploration of such combinations.

3 Conceptual Model of ProxSituated Visualization

In this section, we reiterate and adapt existing models of situated visualization to differentiate between proxy and physical referents explicitly.

3.1 Data and physical referent in Situated Visualization

We start out by investigating the role of data in situated visualization. A definitive understanding of data in situated visualization will later help to define what constitutes proxsituated visualization. We observe the following:

In situated visualization, data and its visualization are the driving factors, while the physical referent is what makes the analysis situated.

Data in situated visualization is data that has an association with physical objects or spaces in the world. Theoretical discussions of situated visualization, such as those of White [ 97 ] and Willett et al. [ 100 ], emphasize relationships between data, data representation (Data Rep), and physical referent (Ref). The relationship between the data and its physical referent (Figure 2 , Ref  −  Data ) can be semantic or spatial [ 97 ] and can be stored explicitly or implicitly in the data set [ 100 ]. To perceive the data, we need to transform it into a form of representation. Data representation is the output of the visualization pipeline [ 14 ], which is one or multiple charts presented on a display (Figure 2 , Data  −  Data Rep ). We refer to data and its visualization as data visualization. The relationship between the data visualization and the physical referent can be characterized by their spatial and temporal indirectness [ 100 ] (Figure 2 , Data Rep  −  Ref ), which we will revisit later.

Without the presence of a physical referent, data visualization is considered non-situated. Analyzing supermarket product data at an office separated from the supermarket (as illustrated by Willett et al. [ 100 ]), and analyzing site visit data at the analyst office (as illustrated in the SiteLens paper [ 95 ]), physically disconnect the visualization from its physical referent. That is to say, the driving factor of situated visualization is the intention to understand the data through visualization, and it is better achieved by the presence of the physical referent. Imagine again supermarket data, but where the analysis task is to understand sales data, which may be affected by size, packaging, or position of products on shelves. What constitutes situated visualization is then a data visualization that allows for user observation of the physical referent. A simple model 2 of situated visualization would then consist of data visualization and physical referent as shown in Figure 3 .

Suppose we have a data set of factory machines’ performance, and the observations in the data set have a semantic association with actual machines in the factory. Visualizing the machine's performance in the proximity of the machine, for instance, using mobile AR, can be described by the model of situated visualization shown in Figure 3 . The data visualization results in the visual form of the machine's performance data on the mobile screen (e.g., using bar charts), and the physical referent is the machine. How about the environment around the machine?

3.2 Environment in Situated Visualization

We start our extension of Willett et al.’s [ 100 ] model by discussing the environment around a physical referent, which is not explicitly covered in the models in Figures 2 and 3 . We define “environment” as the surroundings of a physical referent. Physical objects, social interaction, or non-visual aspects such as temperature or noise are part of the environment and are often not directly related to, or recorded in, the data. The relationship we observed in this situation is the one between the physical referent and its environment, as opposed to the relationship between the data and the environment. We argue the following:

The environment around the physical referent is often intrinsic to the situated visualization process.

Adopting the figure-ground [ 93 ] concept, we can say that the physical referent is the figure and the environment is the ground . The environment around the physical referent can provide information beyond semantic meaning in the data set. As we deal with the physical world, environmental factors co-exist with the physical referent in most cases. Referring back to the factory machine data set, the size of the machine, the physical features, and the sound or temperature of the machine are information that is intrinsic to the physical referent. Whereas the other nearby machines, factory ambient sound, or workers’ interactions in the factory are part of the environment and are naturally experienced in conjunction with the physical referent. A surgeon viewing a situated visualization of a patient's brain data would not only encounter the patient's head as the physical referent but also the patient's body features, as well as the room's lighting and objects in the surgery room. A situated visualization of supermarket products embeds environmental conditions of the lane on which the products are located. This pattern, however, is not without exceptions, as an unbounded physical referent can change its natural environment. For instance, a situated visualization of a product's sales data when the product is in the warehouse might not contain meaningful environmental factors. Yet, we argue that an intrinsic environment factor is common in situated visualization. Building on that argument, we can extend the model of Figure 3 into the one shown in Figure 4 .

One might argue that the environment is part of the physical referent as it contributes contextual meaning to the data visualization process. While that is a fair argument, our separation of physical referent from its physical environment also serves as a foundation for the conceptual model of proxsituated visualization described later.

3.3 Situatedness in Situated Visualization

From Figure 4 , we can see that removing the physical referent and the environment from the model would leave us with non-situated data visualization. This can imply that the “situatedness” in situated visualization is mainly contributed by the presence of the physical referent and its environment. In Figure 5 we represent an extended model that includes situatedness from which we start our definition of the proxsituated visualization conceptual model.

In situated visualization, the presence of a physical referent and the environment forms situatedness. We argue that the actual referent and environment create immediate situatedness , while representations of physical referent and environment create proxsituatedness . 3.4 Entities in ProxSituated Visualization

The immediate versus proxied situated visualization shown in the model in Figure 6 can be described in more detail by looking at entities and the relationships between entities involved in proxsituated visualization. We present our extended model in Figure 7 . Our proposed model considers three worlds: logical world , physical world , and proxy world . In line with Willett et al.’ model, the logical world consists of raw data and a visualization pipeline. The physical world is the location in which the immediate physical referent is located. This is where immediate situated visualization takes place. The proxy world is where proxsituated visualization takes place. This includes the world in which proxsituatedness is experienced (e.g., immersive virtual environment) and the user's workspace (e.g., at the office). For example, when a user is performing proxsituated visualization in an immersive world using a virtual reality display, their movement might be limited by their physical workspace. This dynamic interaction happens in the proxy world.

In our model, the data representation can exist in physical and proxy worlds. Physical referent ( Ref ) and the environment ( Env ) are in the physical world, while their proxies ( Ref Prox and Env Prox ) exist in the proxy world through a set of relationships represented by arrows Ref  −  Ref Prox and Env  −  Env Prox on the figure. In the physical world, the physical referent and environment tend to co-exist naturally (as described in section 3.2). This is not the case for proxsituated visualization, where the relationship between physical referent proxy and environment proxy ( Ref Prox  −  Env Prox ) does not have hard spatial constraints. The data representation in immediate situated visualization is mapped through Data Rep  −  Ref relationship, while in proxsituated visualization, it is associated with the physical referent proxy through the arrow Data Rep  −  Ref Prox .

To summarise, relationships Ref  −  Data , Data  −  Data Rep , and Data Rep  −  Ref , and the following entities are from Willett et al.’ model [ 100 ]. Definitions of existing entities are as follows.

  1. Raw Data : the data related to physical objects or spaces.
  2. Visualization Pipeline : a set of steps to encode data into visual graphics [ 14 ].
  3. Data Representation : the output of the visualization pipeline presented on a display.
  4. Physical Referent : a physical entity or a group of physical entities to which the data refers.

In our extended model, we introduce relationships

Data Rep  −  Ref Prox , Ref  −  Env Prox , Ref Prox  −  Env Prox ,

Ref  −  Ref Prox , and Env  −  Env Prox , and the following entities.

  1. Environment : the space and all physical entities around the immediate physical referent.
  2. Physical Referent Proxy : the proxy representation of the immediate physical referent.
  3. Environment Proxy : the proxy representation of the immediate environment.

In the next sections, we will describe the relationships between entities in our model. We suggest looking at Figure 7 in more detail before moving on to the next section.

3.5 Relationships between entities in the model

Two entities can be connected through a set of relationships. In the existing model [ 100 ], the relationships between data representation and the physical referent ( Data Rep  −  Ref relationships) are described using spatial indirection and temporal indirection . These indirection factors represent offset in space and time. The two relationships were borrowed from an existing user interface framework [ 4 ]. We adapt these relationships into our extended model and introduce two additional relationships: representation scale and representation form . The details are as follows.

3.5.1 Spatial Indirection. Spatial indirection is an offset of space between two entities. For example, charts embedded next to the physical referent have a low spatial indirection. The higher the spatial offset, the higher the indirection. Willett et al. [ 100 ] described how spatial indirection applies to their model. In this section, we only focus on spatial indirection in the following relationships: Data Rep  −  Ref Prox , Ref Prox  −  Env Prox , Ref  −  Ref Prox , and Env  −  Env Prox .

The Data Rep  −  Ref Prox represents relationships between data representation and physical referent proxy. Similar to immediate situated visualization, low spatial indirection in proxsituated visualization would mean that the data representation is embedded close to the physical referent proxy. This can be achieved by encoding value to the referent's properties, such as color coding or embedding charts into the physical referent proxy. For example, registering virtual charts to scale models of physical referents in augmented reality would have low spatial indirection. Using a similar proxy, presenting charts on a hand-held tablet would have higher spatial indirection.

The Ref Prox  −  Env Prox arrow in our model represents the relationships between the physical referent proxy and the environment proxy. As described earlier, the environment in the immediate situated visualization tends to be intrinsic in the visualization process. It means in most cases that the spatial indirection between the physical referent and its environment is low. This is not the case for proxsituated visualization. The relationships indicated by Ref Prox  −  Env Prox in proxsituated visualization allow for tightly coupled spatial relationships or spatial separation between physical referent proxy and environment proxy. An example of low spatial indirectness would be a scale model of a building (Ref Prox) placed on top of a tabletop map (Env Prox), as shown in Figure 8 -a. When the scales of the building model and the map are different, such as shown in Figure 8 -b, the two entities can be separated in space and have higher spatial indirectness.

Relationships Ref  −  Ref Prox and Env  −  Env Prox represent the relationships between immediate situatedness and proxsituatedness. These relationships associate the physical referent and the environment with their proxies. In terms of spatial indirection, scenarios with a low spatial indirectness would have a physical referent proxy and environment proxy in the proximity of their immediate counterparts. An example would be a proxsituated visualization of a campus building performed in the same building. Proxsituated visualization in a different location than the immediate location will have higher spatial indirectness. For example, proxsituated visualization of the same campus building performed in another city. In this case, high spatial indirectness does not mean the visualization approach is non-situated, as the presence of a physical referent proxy provides proxsituatedness.

It is worth mentioning that spatial indirection can be measured physically or perceptually [ 90 , 100 ]. The examples mentioned in this section mainly focus on the physical offset. For more information about perceptual offset, see Thomas et al.’ book chapter [ 90 ].

3.5.2 Temporal Indirection. Temporal indirection is a time offset between two entities. This has also been described in Willett et al.’ model. For the temporal indirectness in this section, we highlight Data  −  Data Rep , Ref  −  Ref Prox , and Env  −  Env Prox . These relationships connect data, physical referent, and environment to their representations: data representation, physical referent proxy, and environment proxy.

We can roughly discretize the temporal indirection of representations relative to their immediate counterpart into historical , live , or predictive depending on the state of the real-world existence of the represented aspects. Live representation has the lowest temporal indirection, whereas historical and predictive represents higher temporal indirection. In respect of mentioned relationships, data representation can show historical data, live stream data, or predictive data. Similarly, physical referent proxy and environment proxy can also be based on the historical state of the objects, live state (e.g., using streaming of volumetric video), or predictive state (e.g., design of a new building). Apart from historical, live, and predictive, the temporal relationship can also be based on synthesized objects and data. For example, data sets or objects designed for user study purposes can be completely fictional (e.g., in Löchtefeld et al.  [ 53 ]).

Beyond the mentioned relationships, it is worth briefly discussing Data Rep  −  Ref Prox and Ref Prox  −  Env Prox relationships in the model. While these relationships can be described using temporal indirection, we see that an alternate presence between the entities is only reasonable for supporting user interaction in a complex system. For instance, AR charts (Data Rep) embedded next to a scale model (Ref Prox) can be shown or hidden to reduce visual clutter (e.g., in Prozeau et al. [ 72 ]) but showing charts and the physical referent independently in time is not an ideal design as it may break the integration between data representation and the referent. This also applies to the relationship between the physical referent proxy (Ref Prox) and the environment proxy (Env Prox), which is, arguably, better presented concurrently.

3.5.3 Representation Scale. Representation scale is a transformation in scale between two entities. We group the representation scale into two categories: representative fraction (geometric scale) and level of detail (semantic scale). The representation scale can be up-scaled, down-scaled, or real scale. For the representation scale, we illustrate Data  −  Data Rep , Ref  −  Ref Prox and Env  −  Env Prox , and Ref Prox  −  Env Prox relationships. The scale relationship between data and its representation (Data Rep) only includes the level of details aspect. The geometric scale between data and data representation is not sensible as data does not have any physical form. Data representation can show a down-scaled version of the entire data. For instance, by representing the filtered or aggregated value of the data. Real-scale data representation will show a higher level of detail. The up-scaled representation, on the other hand, can show interpolation or prediction.

The Ref  −  Ref Prox and Env  −  Env Prox relationships represent the connection between immediate situatedness and proxsituatedness and can be described using geometric scale and semantic scale. A large immediate physical referent and environment can be geometrically down-scaled. Small immediate physical referent and environment, such as ant colony visualization [ 44 ], can be up-scaled. The real-scale physical referent can also be used in some cases. Semantic scaling can be applied to any case. The semantic scaling between environment and environment proxy might be useful for applying figure-ground [ 93 ] principle by reducing distractions from unnecessary environmental factors, such as shown in Figure 8 -c.

The Ref Prox  −  Env Prox relationship describes the connection between the physical referent proxy and environment proxy. The scale between the two can have different geometric scales and levels of detail with their proxies. For instance, the map and building in Figure 8 -b have different geometric scales. The example in Figure 8 -c can also be seen as having different semantic scales.

3.5.4 Representation Form. Representation forms are the form factor of the representations. We focus on tangibility (e.g., digital vs physical). Representations of data, physical referent, and environment ( Data  −  Data Rep , Ref  −  Ref Prox , and Env  −  Env Prox ) can have various forms. A scenario where immersive AR charts (Data Rep) are embedded next to physical scale models (Ref Prox) has digital data representation and tangible referent proxy. Whereas a color-coded physical scale model is a physical form of data representation and physical referent. While our initial observation of existing work suggests that representation mainly considers visual form, non-visual forms such as sound, temperature, or lighting could also be applied to environment representations.

The spatial indirection, temporal indirection, representation scale, and representation form relationships in our extended situated visualization model provide a conceptual framework to describe entities in the logical world, physical world, and proxy world. The next section provides a further illustration of immediate and proxied situated visualization seen through the perspective of our model.

3.6 Example scenarios

Our illustration of the model is inspired by a real use-case scenario of a steel processing shop floor from an industry partner. The situation of the shop floor is monitored as it is part of a shipbuilding project with hard deadlines. There are numerous machines or workstations (Ref) whose data are collected regularly. The raw data includes various multivariate tables per workstation, such as breakdowns, performance, and maintenance. The production team, consisting of data analysts and manufacturing engineers, needs to inspect the data and obtain an understanding of the past, current, and predicted future situation of the shop floor. The current workflow involves non-situated visualization where the team analyzes the data at their office by exploring representations of data using charts with no context of the physical objects and environment to which the data relates (Figure 1 -a). How would such a scenario translate into immediate situated and proxsituated visualization?

3.6.1 Immediate Situated Visualization Scenario. The immediate situated visualization would require the analyst to analyze the data on the shop floor. Suppose we use AR technology in this scenario. The charts (Data Ref) representing historical, live, or predictive data can then be shown near each machine (Ref), and the analyst is surrounded by the context in the environment (Env) involving, but not limited to, the following: ambient sound, temperature, production activities, interactions between workers interaction, and shop floor lighting conditions. The presence of machines and the environmental context around them provide immediate situatedness (Figure 1 -b).

3.6.2 ProxSituated Visualization Scenarios. Data visualization designers have several options for proxsituated visualization of this scenario. A simple case of proxsituated visualization would be, for example, showing a 3D model of the machines (Ref Prox) in conjunction with the charts on the desktop display (Data Rep), as shown in Figure 1 -c. In the illustration, we see that the environment representation (Env Prox) is absent; a possible design with proxsituated visualization. Since the representation is digital, it is possible to interactively change the scale ( Ref  −  Ref Prox ) of the physical referent proxy.

We take two more advanced examples from existing work, Uplift [ 27 ] and Corsican Twin [ 72 ], whose physical referent proxy varies in representation forms ( Ref  −  Ref Prox ): physical vs digital. An Uplift-like system of the scenario would have scale models of machines (Ref Prox is historical temporal indirectness, down-scaled representation, and is in the physical form) placed on a digital map (Env Prox is historical , down-scale , digital ) of the shop floor (low spatial indirection of Ref Prox  −  Env Prox ). This is illustrated in Figure 1 -d. The Corsican Twin version, on the other hand, would have an immersive digital model of the machines and their surroundings (Ref Proxy and Env Prox are historical , real-scale , and digital ), as shown in Figure 1 -e.

Our envisioned proxsituated visualization goes beyond the two examples described above. To illustrate, we could modify the physical referent proxy from the Uplift-like system by employing the “immersive macroscopic view” [ 58 , 84 ] technique. This would allow for a first-person view of the scale model (Figure 1 -f). Furthermore, this approach can be combined with a mini avatar [ 71 ] to push the non-traditional user experience even further. The complex mixture of representation techniques and technologies poses an interesting unknown area for exploration.

4 UNDERSTANDING STATE OF THE ART

To better understand the current state of the art of proxsituated visualization, we illustrate how a design space can be derived from our model and then synthesize existing proxsituated visualization work.

4.1 An illustration of design space

A design space informs the design practice by representing different rationales for designing a certain type of artifacts and can be described by questions, options, and criterion factors [ 54 ]. A design space of proxsituated visualization can be generated from the proposed model's entities and relationships. For example, one question could be “what is the temporal indirection of the physical referent proxy relative to the immediate referent?”. As described earlier, the options are historical, live, predictive, and synthesized. The criterion for the live option, for example, is that it requires a live stream of the physical properties of the physical referent. Applying this approach to entities and relationships in our model, we can generate a multi-dimensional design space of proxsituated visualization. Figure 9 illustrates a slice of this design space. This illustration comprises four main categories, each of which is the type of relationship in our model. In each relationship, we present an example of an entity pair (left box with a solid line border) and an example of possible options (right box with a dashed line border). For instance, the first item in the spatial relationship category looks at the spatial indirection between the immediate physical referent (Ref) and physical referent proxy (Ref Proxy). This can range from low spatial indirection to high spatial indirection. Of course, more pairs of entities can be described similarly. This pattern can be applied to other relationships. See Figure 9 for a better overview of the examples.

The application of the relationship between entities in the design space is not limited to a single option; we can use multiple options for the same design question. Revisiting the temporal indirection question in the previous paragraph, it is possible to mix historical, live, and predictive referent proxies in the same application. For example, we can combine physical scale models of campus buildings with a scale model of a planned building or even with a live stream of the construction process. A similar mixed approach can also apply to spatial indirection, representation form, and representation scale.

What can we learn from the existing work through the proxsituated visualization design space? In the remainder of this section, we explore the design space's capability to describe existing work, evaluate design alternatives, and generate novel design ideas [ 5 ]. Our method follows a semi-systematic review [ 86 ]. We collected papers that could fall into proxsituated visualization, then synthesized their content to understand the stated themes in literature as well as establish a future research agenda.

4.2 Review method

Following existing work [ 7 ], we used the seed papers approach as our paper collection method. We went through the corpus from a recent literature review of situated visualization by Bressa et al. [ 7 ] and data physicalization by Bae et al. [ 3 ]. We also searched through SiteLens [ 95 ] paper citations as well as citations and references of Uplift [ 27 ], Corsican Twin [ 72 ] papers. We obtained the list of citations from the Scopus database iteratively between early June and early August 2022. Our main inclusion criteria are “the use of representations of physical objects or spaces”. We excluded survey and theoretical papers we found (i.e., [ 7 , 8 , 16 , 25 , 26 , 33 , 35 , 36 , 40 , 43 , 64 , 69 , 77 , 80 , 90 , 99 , 104 ]). We picked the latest work from two papers with similar visualizations [ 51 , 52 ]. We added three recently published papers that are not captured by the database (we refer to them as “augmentation” [ 3 ]). Figure 10 shows the PRISMA chart [ 68 ] of our paper collection process.

Our final corpus consists of 39 papers from 26 unique venues ranging from the year 1999 to 2022 (Figure 11 ). The list of papers is in the Appendix and Supplementary Material. The comparison with situated visualization papers 3 shows that proxsituated visualization takes a relatively small part of the literature but shows an increasing trend.

A couple of papers [ 51 , 81 ] contain multiple distinctive examples of visualizations. In such cases, we selected representative visualizations and reviewed them individually. This resulted in 43 proxsituated visualizations from 39 papers. We read through each paper in our corpus, compiled a literature matrix, as well as noted important observations.

The following sections highlight the findings of the review. First, we describe the trend of the use of the term “situated visualization”. Then, we group important findings in regard to spatial indirection, temporal indirection, representation scale, and representation form.

4.3 How is the term Situated Visualization used?

A notable number of papers (31%, shown in Figure 12 ) in our corpus use the term “situated visualization” explicitly with two different contexts of uses while the remaining papers did not mention situated visualization but use representations of objects or spaces. The majority of these papers (9/12) [ 27 , 37 , 39 , 45 , 46 , 49 , 78 , 98 , 105 ] use the term “situated visualization” to describe proxy representations. For instance, Lee et al. [ 45 ] use digital models of spaces and objects to visualize COVID-19 simulation data and state, “ We provided situated visualization that depicts virus transmission on the 3D building model in real-time ”. The other less common context of usage is related to authoring [ 72 ], simulating [ 74 ], and prototyping [ 51 ] immediate situated visualization; these works mix immediate and proxsituated visualizations. For example, Prouzeau et al. [ 72 ] used proxsituated visualization as an authoring tool for immediate situated visualization. They state, ” Our prototype supports several classes of in situ data displays, including both situated and embedded visualizations ”.

4.4 How is spatial indirection applied?

We first highlight spatial indirection between the physical referent proxy and environment proxy ( Ref Prox  −  Env Prox ). Most work (67%) replicates the spatial relationship in the immediate situation, which is mainly low spatial indirection. For instance, building models are placed on top of a map. Despite that, there are cases (21%) where the referent is a space, and thus, the referent representation and environment representation are not differentiable. Examples include visualization of weather data where the entire area, including geographic features in it (e.g., terrain, trees), are part of the physical referent [ 37 ].

Next, we highlight spatial indirection between the physical referent and its proxy ( Ref  −  Ref Prox ). A notable number of works is designed for remote use (high spatial indirection), such as prototyping and authoring immediate situated visualizations [ 51 , 73 ], demonstration purposes [ 98 ], and enhancing sport broadcasting [ 49 ]. There are also a few cases where the physical world encloses the proxy of the world. Figure 13 -a shows a situated visualization scenario where the user can see the terrain (Ref) while holding a proxy of that terrain [ 46 ]. Proxsituated visualization in Figure 13 -b can be performed in the immediate campus, but the users cannot directly observe the proxied buildings [ 27 ]. Note that a spatial offset between the physical referent and its proxy is only possible for existing physical objects or space (i.e., historical and temporal indirectness). A predictive proxy, in general, does not have a spatial relationship with the immediate referent, as a physical referent has yet to exist. However, we found a case [ 83 ] where a predictive physical referent is visualized in the immediate environment (Figure 13 -c).

In terms of the spatial indirectness relationship between data representation and physical referent proxy ( Data Rep  −  Ref Prox ), the data representation (Data Rep) is mainly integrated with or placed very close to the physical referent proxy (Ref Prox). This shows that embedded visualization [ 100 ] is predominant.

4.5 How is temporal indirection applied?

First, we highlight the temporal indirection between the physical referent and its proxy ( Ref  −  Ref Prox ). Historical proxy representation of physical referent is the most common (77%) use cases. This is reasonable considering that most physical referents in our corpus are static objects and spaces (e.g., buildings, space, terrain) that do not change in shape frequently. Though we found instances [ 48 , 51 ] of historical proxy of sports visualization that changes dynamically over a times-span. Predictive physical referent proxy is common in architecture design work (e.g., [ 2 , 83 , 92 ]) with a few instances simulating of virus spread [ 45 ] and demonstrating fueling process [ 98 ]. Synthesized physical referent proxy is not inspired by actual objects or spaces. The use of this type of proxy includes, but is not limited to user study purposes [ 53 ], demonstration of a visualization toolkit [ 53 ], and educational purposes [ 91 ]. Live physical referent proxy requires data streaming and is rare in our corpus. One example is a system for authoring AR experience, CAVE-AR [ 11 ]. The system allows for a live video stream from a mobile device situated outdoors to be displayed on a large display placed indoors in another location. See Figure 14 -top for examples of work in different temporal indirection groups and Figure 14 -bottom for the distribution of work concerning temporal indirection.

In terms of temporal indirection between raw data and data representation ( Data  −  Data Rep ), historical representation is a predominant use case, while live, predictive, and synthesized share similar trends (Figure 14 -bottom). Predictive data representation includes building related-data [ 31 , 83 , 92 ] (e.g., simulated shadow, lighting, wind flow), simulated urban indicators data [ 2 , 56 ], simulated COVID-19 data [ 45 ], and simulated data related to hydrogen fuel [ 98 ].

Combining temporal indirection between function Ref  −  Ref Prox and Data  −  Data Rep creates a two-dimensional design space that illustrates the trend and gap (Figure 14 -bottom). The combination between historical indirection for relationship Data  −  Data Rep and Ref  −  Ref Prox is predominant (39%). ProxSituated is mostly used to visualize historical data in conjunction with physical referent representations created from historical information. Another trend is for predictive data representation and physical referent proxy, which covers works on architecture [ 83 , 92 ], urban design [ 2 , 56 ], process demonstration [ 31 , 98 ], or physical object design [ 28 ].

Temporal indirection between environment and its proxy

( Env  −  Env Prox ) shows a similar trend where the historical environment proxy is predominant. We observed that most of the examples use maps (e.g.,  [ 27 , 39 , 53 , 53 , 74 , 85 , 103 ]) or 3D models of existing environments (e.g.,  [ 11 , 24 , 38 , 51 , 72 , 81 , 105 ]).

4.6 How is representation scale applied?

First, we highlight Ref  −  Ref Prox and Env  −  Env Prox relationships. Down-scaled physical referent shows a trend (44%), followed by real-scale and dynamic scale that share the same proportion (28%). The down-scaled physical referent proxy is mainly large objects such as buildings or geographical areas. Real-scaled proxy is mainly small to medium size objects such as ATMs, furniture, humans, or fuel pumps. Though in rare occasions, large objects such as buildings are represented in a first-person perspective (Figure 15 -b), or a 1:1 copy of the physical referent is brought to the proxy world (Figure 15 -c). Dynamic scale is an interactive zoomable representation. Upscaling geometric size approach is rare. One use case is where the relative size difference between the physical and environment is large. For example, IoT sensors in an immersive environment. In that case, the sensors are represented as 3D widgets large enough to be easily manipulated by the user (Figure 15 -a). In terms of the size of the environment proxy (i.e., Env  −  Env Prox ), we observed that it is tightly coupled with the size physical referent proxy.

The level of details used to represent the physical referent and its environment are mainly basic geometry, texture, and lighting, rather than hyper-realistic representation. A few exceptions that are more realistic than others include a physical replica of the physical referent [ 98 ] and, possibly, immersive 360 videos [ 51 ].

The semantic scale transformation between the raw data and data representation ( Data  −  Data Rep ) was not straightforward to identify. This is because most works did not specify the structure of the entire database. Though we could identify around 26% of work down-scaled the amount of data using filtering and aggregation techniques. A small number of works show qualitative data such as documentation of physical objects [ 28 ] or campus safety information [ 15 ], which was presented without semantic scaling.

4.7 How is representation form applied?

First, we describe referent representation forms of physical referent proxy and environment proxy ( Ref  −  Ref Prox and Env  −  Env Prox ). The main form of physical referent proxy is a digital artifact (74%), followed by physical (21%), and a couple of works (5%) that combine digital and physical referent proxy. The environment representation is mainly digital (e.g., maps, CAD models, immersive virtual environments). In terms of display technology, we identify various display technologies used to experience the proxsituatedness. Head-worn VR has the largest amount of share (33%), followed by the physical scale model (19%) and hand-held AR (12%). Desktop, large display, and head-worn AR share a similar trend at about 5%. The rest of the displays (22.5%) are a combination of multiple sensing technologies. We also observed one shape-changing physical referent proxy, which represents an interactive physical terrain visualization [ 29 ].

Most data representations are digital. A few cases show digitally color-coded tangible physical referent proxy, forming data physicalization. Examples include a combination between the semi-transparent physical scale models (Ref Prox) and a tabletop display or a projection-based AR (Data Rep). Most work (80%) use the same digital modality to represent the referent and the data representation, and only a small part of the examples (20%) used a combination of physical and digital forms for these entities (e.g., [ 39 , 92 , 98 ]). We also found a case where the proxsituated application allows for cross-modality transitions between desktop and immersive virtual environments [ 38 ].

5 DISCUSSION

So far, we have gathered insights into the state of existing proxsituated visualization work. We used the combination of entities and relationships to position these existing works. In this section, we provide a summary of our findings and highlight research gaps and future directions.

5.1 Summary of state of the art

Using a design space derived from our model, we analyzed 43 existing proxsituated visualizations. While the proxsituated visualization work remains relatively small compared to the whole situated visualization literature, we observed an increasing trend, making the introduction of our proposed model timely (Figure 11 ). We describe the state of existing work using the dimensions of our design space (Section 3 ):

  • Spatial indirection . Most work uses proxies for the spatial indirection between referent and environment, for instance, digital models in an immersive environment or scale models on top of a map. We also observed variation in spatial offsets, such as high spatial offset proxsituated visualization designed for remote use (e.g., remote VR replication of a physical environment), work with low spatial indirection where physical referent encloses its proxy (e.g., visualizing campus data in the same campus), as well as low spatial indirection where predictive physical referent proxy is placed over the immediate environment (e.g., presenting building design on the site using mobile AR). There is a clear trend in preserving the physical integration between data representation and physical referent proxy by embedding the data representation into or around the referent proxy.
  • Temporal indirection . Most works use the proxy to represent historical raw data, referent, and environment. A fair amount of work uses a predictive proxy representation to design objects or spaces. Although live proxy representations are rare, there are a few instances where historical data and proxies are presented in a specific time span to simulate live events.
  • Representation scale . Downscaling a large referent and environment is a predominant use case, followed by a similar share between real and interactive scales. Our observation shows that realistic physical referent proxy and environment proxy are rare, with most representations using non-photorealistic geometry, texture, and lighting. The scale of data is not always clearly stated, but we observed variations of aggregated and non-aggregated data.
  • Representation form . Digital representations are a predominant choice, followed by tangible, and a mix of digital and tangible. While VR is the most common type of display, its share is less than half of the use cases. We found a mix of display technology used, including tangible scale models, AR displays, large displays, and desktop displays. Most data representation and physical referent proxy combinations use the same display modality.

5.2 Research gaps and future directions

We highlight several gaps and future directions from what we have learned through the review of existing work.

5.2.1 Combining immediate situated and proxsituated visualization. We observed that proxsituated visualization is largely performed without the existence of immediate visualization. Raw data and physical referent data are collected, brought to the proxy world, and analyzed with a proxsituated visualization approach. A few examples using both approaches concurrently do exist but were designed for authoring purposes rather than data analytics. In the future, combining immediate and proxsituated visualization for data analysis should be explored, both in single-user or multiple-user scenarios. We envision a multiple-user scenario where users in the field observe the physical referent directly with associated data and simultaneously visualize the associated data via physical referents remotely. In cases of the referent being mobile, robotic-enhanced tangibles could be employed [ 76 ]. Furthermore, from a single immediate physical world, there could be multiple proxy worlds with different techniques. This one-to-many relationship is worth exploring as it enables novel scenarios.

5.2.2 Designing live proxsituated visualization. Live representation of data and referent is an apparent gap in our corpus. Note that the term “live” in this case means there is some sort of live data streaming process. Live representations would be ideal for dynamic physical referents such as sports players. Technical issues could be the main contributor to such scarcity. One of the works in our corpus on sport proxsituated visualization pointed out issues with live data streams, including data transmission delay, incomplete data, and restricted access to API [ 49 ]. While a simple live physical referent proxy presented as a video stream was demonstrated, live 3D representations of physical referent and environment will bring a different level of proxsituatedness as well as design and technical challenges for future research.

5.2.3 Integrating more data with physical referent proxy. We observed a variety of types of data representation based on the amount of encoded data. Figure 16 illustrates this observation. In some cases, the data only represents information required to construct the representation of the physical referent. We refer to this scenario as Physical only in the Figure. A single data attribute—typically derived from aggregation—can be encoded directly to the physical properties of the physical referent. For instance, color coding of physical referent proxy is a common pattern we observed. Increasing the amount of data would require a dedicated data representation object. Wind flow simulation or bars on top of scale models, for instance, embed marks around the referent representation. This approach allows for data encoding multiple attributes to the visual channels of the mark, e.g., height, color, or scale, without affecting the physical property of the referent proxy. However, the embedded mark is often limited to a single mark per referent. More complex data, such as values over time, would require animation or a chart with dedicated axes and multiple marks embedded next to the referent proxy. We refer to this technique as an embedded chart. Lastly, multiple charts presented in a dashboard can also be embedded next to the referent proxy, increasing the amount of encoded data even further.

In our corpus, the embedded mark approach is the most common form of data representation, while the embedded dashboard is the least used technique in our corpus. A few examples of embedded dashboards explore textual information [ 28 , 94 ] and only recently, multivariate data [ 49 , 78 ]. For future work, it is worth exploring the amount of data that can be integrated with a physical referent proxy before the visualization becomes overly complex to read. For instance, a recent work on augmented scale model [ 78 ] used eight charts in their study, but the task was rather controlled, as opposed to purely exploratory visual analysis.

5.2.4 Proxsituating data physicalization. The interaction between data representation and physical referent proxy from the representation form (i.e., none, digital, and physical) reveals a matrix of techniques shown in Figure 17 . This combination of forms can be categorized into three groups. First is the “Ref Prox Only” group, consisting of visualization techniques that only represent objects or spaces (e.g., 3D model, scale model). The second group is “Data Rep Only”. This group consists of information visualization and data physicalization. The third group is the combination of data representation and physical referent proxy, the “Data Rep and Ref Prox” group.

Techniques used are predominantly “digital data representation and physical referent proxy” and “digital data representation and physical-physical referent proxy”. Whereas physical data representation combined with digital or physical-physical referent proxy is less common in our corpus. Situated data physicalization placed in an immediate environment has been demonstrated, as seen in a recent data physicalization review [ 3 ]. However, proxsituating the physical representation of data opens up opportunities for new creative designs. For example, physical bar charts representing machine breakdowns can be augmented with a digital representation of the referent proxy (Figure 18 -a). Another example is a fully physical approach that combines physical bar charts and physical scale models, as illustrated in Figure 18 -b. Exploring designs for such proxsituated data physicalizations can make an interesting future direction.

5.2.5 Designing immersive environment proxy. In some cases, non-visual properties of the environment, such as sound, temperature, or smell, can be beneficial. For instance, in an immediate situated visualization of a factory machine, these properties can act as cues for experts to recognize issues such as wear and tear. Similarly, in a crime scene, an immediate situated visualization requires the understanding of the temperature of the environment that can help interpret forensic evidence, such as the time required for blood to dry [ 75 ]. These complex non-visual cues have not been utilized in proxsituated visualization. We observe a few occasions where representation includes sounds, such as to simulate ambient noise around an ATM [ 60 ] or as part of sports games [ 51 ]. However, non-visual environmental factors such as temperature or smell have not been represented, potentially due to the challenges of data collection and representation of these properties. Engineering techniques for collecting and presenting such information and a wider synthesis of use cases where non-visual environment context is useful will benefit future proxsituated visualizations.

5.2.6 Weaving related research fields. ProxSituated visualization can be related to other existing domains, for example, Digital Twin. Künz et al.[ 43 , p. 3] defines a digital twin as “ the virtual representation of a physical asset presenting information about its current state ”. A recent survey on digital twins and AR found that the majority of work superimposes data representation on immediate physical referent [ 43 ]. Another digital twin review [ 70 ] looks at an immersive virtual reality, where they found cases for remote collaboration, training, data collection, and user studies. Most examples were heavily focused on physical referents with only limited work exploring larger amounts of data representation, such as those of Prouzeau et al. [ 72 ]. Observing increasingly popular technology such as AR and VR in domain-specific visualization such as manufacturing or architecture, it is useful to think about existing paradigms such as Digital Twin, Dual Reality [ 47 ], or World in Miniature [ 19 , 87 ] through the lens of proxsituated visualization to understand the extent of situated visualization better. Our current perspective sees proxsituated visualization as a broad concept whose key point is the use of “proxy representation of physical referent”. This could include but is not limited to, digital twins. For instance, physical scale models are also part of proxsituated visualization. Nevertheless, we hope future research will challenge and refine our current understanding of proxsituated visualization.

6 LIMITATIONS

We acknowledge the limitations of our data collection approach. While our approach is systematic, reproducible, and within-situated visualization work, we are aware that there is work beyond and within-situated visualization that could be incorporated into the review. This includes urban data visualization projects such as the CityScope [ 34 ]. Nevertheless, our corpus provides initial insights into the topic and can be used as a foundation for more elaborate reviews in the future.

Another limitation of proxsituated visualization as a general concept is that there is a trade-off in the investment in both time and money of fabricating proxies of referents versus either physically going to the actual referent or making do with purely virtual representations. Of course, there are many situations where being physically near the referent is impossible, e.g., if the site is far away or not at a convenient scale (e.g., many building site scenarios), dangerous (e.g., nuclear reactors) or cannot be contaminated (e.g., pharmaceutical manufacturing, medical scenarios or crime scenes). The cost trade-off versus virtual representations is very technology dependent. For example, it is only in recent years that advances in rapid fabrication techniques, such as 3D printing and computer numerical control, have made many of the scenarios considered in our paper viable. We can expect future technologies such as robotics (e.g., [ 18 ]) to tip the scales further toward the viability of proxy representations. This cost-benefit trade-off was not part of the focus of our review, but it is worth investigating in the future.

7 CONCLUSION

Situated visualization has introduced a means of understanding data using visualization placed in the proximity of physical referents. Since the early 2000s, a large body of situated visualization work has been produced. In recent years, along with the commercialization of AR, VR, and 3D printing technologies, situated visualizations in which the user can interact with proxy representations of physical referents have emerged. This includes the introduction of tools for supporting situated visualization in immediate environment [ 10 , 32 , 65 ] and proxied environment [ 38 ]. We observed the limitation of current situated visualization models and the lack of common vocabulary to describe this type of work. To address this limitation, we propose the term ProxSituated Visualization and an extended situated visualization model. Our extended model of situated visualization groups techniques into immediate situated visualization and proxsituated visualization, introduces entities involved in the visualization workflow, characterizes relationships between them, and defines a multi-dimensional design space.

Through a review of existing literature, we better understand – borrowing Beaudouin-Lafon's evaluation of interaction model [ 5 ] – the descriptive power, evaluative power, and generative power of our model. Descriptive power is the ability to describe existing designs. Our models are capable of describing real-world scenarios (Section 3.6) and existing work (Section 4). Evaluative power is the ability to base the evaluation of existing and new designs. The design space created from our model can be used to evaluate the vast territory of proxsituated visualization. Through the model, we can ask evaluative questions such as: how do representation scales and forms affect our understanding of the data in proxsituated visualization? Generative power is the ability to facilitate the creation of new designs. We demonstrate this through gaps and future directions derived from the review (Section 5.2). This includes, but is not limited to: combining immediate and proxsituated visualization, designing live representations, integrating more data into proxsituated visualization, proxsituating data physicalization, designing immersive environment proxies, and weaving related disciplines.

Our model is expressive enough to describe situated visualization with the proxy of situatedness yet not over-fit to specific examples. Our decision to take a step back and extend Willett et al.’ model allows us to adapt proxsituatedness into its extended models. For instance, we could connect the “representation” term differentiating visualization and physicalization in Willett et al.’ [ 100 ] model to define ProxSituated and ProxEmbedded Representations. The user interaction entity in Thomas et al.’ [ 90 ] model could also be added to our model. Furthermore, situated analytics could be extended into ProxSituated Analytics.

Future studies are necessary to understand the benefits of different proxsituated visualization techniques in anticipation of fast-advancing data collection, data processing, computing, and rendering technologies. Riffing on the seminal idea introduced by Ivan Sutherland of “the ultimate display" [ 88 ], we anticipate a future where it will be possible to create the ultimate proxsituated visualization tool that is capable of seamlessly catering to the gamut of possible scenarios in this multi-dimensional design space. We believe this vision is shared among human-computer interaction researchers, for instance, there has been a recent attempt at a seamless workflow bridging desktop-based and immersive proxsituated visualizations [ 38 ]. Furthermore, a prominent work of the transitional augmented reality interface [ 6 ] is also aligned with this idea. In the future, the technology used to create proxy representations will be extremely advanced such that immediate and proxsituatedness must be made distinguishable through technical implementation rather than user perception. This would mean that such an ultimate tool is not only capable of perfectly simulating immediate situatedness but is also able to manipulate physical objects in various forms and scales depending on the task's requirements. Before we reach that point, it is crucial to be precise in our discussion of immediate and proxy of situated visualization, which will lead us towards answering the fundamental question: if the ultimate proxsituated visualization tool exists, will immediate situated visualization be needed at all?
